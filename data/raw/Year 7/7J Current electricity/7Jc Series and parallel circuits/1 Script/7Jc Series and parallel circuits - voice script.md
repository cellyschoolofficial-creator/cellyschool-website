# 7Jc Series and parallel circuits — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Christmas tree hook** (Jc00): "old Christmas lights in one loop: one blows, all go dark" is ◇ everyday knowledge, not in the book. Many older light strings were wired in series. The script says "some old Christmas lights", not "all".
- ⚑ **True story** (Jc10): the book says only that some of the world's first streetlights were installed in 1878 in London, along the River Thames. The script adds two ◇ facts beyond the book: in 1881 the Savoy Theatre in London became the first public building lit entirely by electric light, with Joseph Swan's bulbs; and in 1882 Edward Johnson, who worked with Thomas Edison, hand-wired 80 red, white and blue bulbs on a Christmas tree in his New York home. The script does not claim Johnson's tree was wired in series.
- ⚑ **Streetlights question** (book Q6): "all the streetlights in a town need to be on or off at the same times; which circuit?" The script answers: parallel, with one switch in the main part of the circuit, so one broken lamp doesn't put the rest out (◇ reasoning; check the answer key).
- **House lights** (book Q2): homes are wired in parallel, so each light can be switched separately.
- **Book numbers:** main current 0.2 A splits into 0.1 A + 0.1 A in two branches (circuit C).
- **Home lab:** foil-and-card switches, 2 AA cells only, a buzzer or small bulb. Never mains. Unclip when finished.
- ◇ **"Series is a single snake. Parallel has paths."**, **"Split and sum."** and **"AND needs all. OR needs one."** are Volty-made tricks.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Jc Series and parallel circuits (p156–157). Celly School's third lesson of 7J Current electricity.
- **Size:** 135 spoken lines (Volty 121, Bolt 14), 27 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1450 words. That is roughly 17–18 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice Autonoe).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ia.

**World:** The Bead Loop gets a junction. The necklace of beads reaches a fork like a motorway splitting into lanes, and the lanes join again later. Turnstiles on each branch and on the main road show the numbers adding up. Later, a bank vault (AND) and a house with two doorbells (OR).
- **Case:** "The case of the dark Christmas tree" (the 7Jb teaser).
- **Answered at the end:** the old lights were wired in series, one single loop. One broken bulb makes a gap, so every bulb goes out. Houses are wired in parallel, so each light has its own path and the others stay on.

**Main tricks:**
- "Series is a single snake. Parallel has paths." One hand slithers in a loop; two hands split apart and rejoin.
- "Split and sum." In parallel, the current splits at the junction, and the branch currents add up again.
- "AND needs all. OR needs one." Two thumbs up for AND, one thumb up for OR.
