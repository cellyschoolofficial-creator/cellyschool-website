# 7Ja Switches and current — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **"Slower than a snail"** (hook, Ja00_03): charges drift very slowly in a wire (◇ about millimetres per second or less). This isn't in the book. It's used as a wonder line in the hook only and never tested. The answer (the wire is already full of charges, so the whole loop moves at once) is the design doc's ◇ model point.
- ⚑ **Galvani and Volta** (true story, Ja10): the book gives only "the original cell was invented by Alessandro Volta in 1800". The Galvani frog story (Bologna, 1780s; legs twitched when touched with two different metals; he called it "animal electricity") is ◇ beyond the book and slightly simplified (Galvani ran many different set-ups). Volta's pile used discs of two metals (zinc with copper or silver) separated by card or cloth soaked in salty water. The script says "two metals" and "salty wet card". Napoleon's honours are left out.
- ⚑ **Volty is named after the volt** (brand doc rule): said once, in Ja10_09, straight after "the volt is named after Volta". Don't repeat it in 7Jb–7Je.
- **Bulb symbol:** the script describes the lamp symbol as "a circle with a cross inside", the standard UK symbol used in the book. Check it matches the book's symbol page if the workbook draws it differently.
- **Book facts used:** current is "the amount of electricity that is flowing around a circuit" and carries energy to components; a cell or power pack provides it; there must be a complete circuit; a switch joins the metal wires; one broken bulb in a single loop puts the other out (a gap); filaments are very thin wires that glow; more bulbs in a loop give out less light; ammeter, amperes (A), position doesn't matter, current not used up, same everywhere; metal wires with plastic coating; torch faults (book Q7).
- **Safety (home lab):** 1–3 AA cells only, a grown-up present, never mains, never a wire straight across a cell (short circuit), button/coin cells away from little children.
- ◇ **"No loop, no light"**, **"Amps count, amps are constant"**, **"Flat, filament, fracture, fault"** and **"The beads come home. The energy does not."** are Volty-made tricks.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Ja Switches and current (p149–151). Celly School's first lesson of 7J Current electricity.
- **Size:** 145 spoken lines (Volty 132, Bolt 13), 24 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1560 words. That is roughly 18–19 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice Autonoe).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ia.

**World:** The Bead Loop. Volty is a spark, so he can slide inside a wire. The wire is packed end to end with glowing blue beads (the charges), like a necklace that fills every wire. The cell is the Pusher. Bulbs are narrow Squeeze Tunnels. Switches are drawbridges. Ammeters are turnstiles that count beads going past.
- **Case:** "The case of the instant light" (the 7Ie teaser).
- **Answered at the end:** the wire is already full of charges. Close the switch and the whole loop starts moving at once, so the bulb lights instantly even though each charge moves slowly.

**Main tricks:**
- "No loop, no light." Draw a circle in the air, then snap the fingers apart.
- "Amps count. Amps are constant." An ammeter counts, and in one loop the count is the same everywhere.
- "Flat, filament, fracture, fault." The torch detective checklist, counted on four fingers.
- "The beads come home. The energy does not."

### Scene 3 · No loop, no light
- **Ja03_01** [clear] There must be a complete circuit, or the current will not flow.
- **Ja03_02** [warm] If there is a gap anywhere, the bulbs stay dark.
- **Ja03_03** [playful] Here is a trick. No loop, no light.
- **Ja03_04** [gentle] Draw a circle in the air with your finger. Now snap your fingers apart. Lights out!
*(long pause)*
- **Ja03_05** [warm] A switch can make a gap, or close it.
- **Ja03_06** [warm] Press the switch, and it joins the metal wires. The current can flow.
- **Ja03_07** [warm] In the Bead Loop, a switch is a drawbridge. Open it, and every bead stops.
- **Ja03_08** [curious] When the drawbridge opens, do only the nearby beads stop?
*(long pause)*
- **Ja03_09** [pleased] No! The whole loop stops at once. Close it, and the whole loop moves.
- **Ja03_10 BOLT** [BOLT: very sure] I only need one wire to the bulb. One is enough!
- **Ja03_11** [gentle] Sorry, Bolt. One wire is not a loop. No loop, no light.

### Scene 4 · Bulbs and filaments
- **Ja04_01** [clear] Many bulbs light up because a thin wire inside them glows.
- **Ja04_02** [warm] This very thin wire is called the filament.
- **Ja04_03** [slow] Fil. A. Ment. Three taps.
*(long pause)*

- **Ja04_05** [warm] The beads squeeze through. They hand over energy, and the filament glows.
- **Ja04_06** [warm] Add more bulbs to the same loop, and all the bulbs give out less light.

- **Ja04_09 BOLT** [BOLT: thinking] The bulb nearest the cell must be brightest. It gets the beads first!

### Scene 5 · Measuring current
