# 7Jb Models for circuits — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **J. J. Thomson** (true story, Jb10): the book says only that in 1897 the British physicist J. J. Thomson discovered that electricity is tiny charges moving through metals. The extra details are ◇ beyond the book: he worked in Cambridge, studied glowing beams in glass tubes with most of the air pumped out, showed the beams were tiny charged particles much smaller than atoms, and won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1906. The script says "charges" throughout, as the book does; "electrons" is said once as a bonus name.
- ⚑ **"Until just over 100 years ago"** (book): written for a book printed after 1997. The script says "for a long time, scientists thought electricity was a liquid", which stays true.
- **Sam's and Nat's models** (hook, scenes 3–4): from the book (drawings A and B). Sam's water wheel model keeps all its water, so its "current" is the same all round. In Nat's marble model, some marbles fall out through a hole, so the "current" gets smaller. Sam's model is better because current is not used up in a real circuit (book Q3–Q6).
- **One ampere** = 6 250 000 000 000 000 000 charges passing every second (book). The script reads it as "six point two five million million million", and shows the full number on screen.
- **Central heating model** (book diagram A): boiler and pump = cell, hot water = charges, pipes = wires, radiator = bulb. The same amount of water flows at X and Y, but the water at Y stores less energy (it is cooler).
- **Train model** (book diagram C): coal mine = cell, trains = charges, coal = energy, factory = bulb. Its crack (◇): the trains seem to deliver all their energy in one go, and real charges don't carry parcels.
- **Rope loop** (home lab, ◇ IOP model): gentle pulling only, never wrapped round fingers or necks.
- ◇ **"Models make predictions. Models have limits."**, **"Boiler is the battery. Radiator is the ray of light."** and **"Behaves like the real thing"** are Volty-made tricks.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Jb Models for circuits (Working Scientifically, p152–153) and 7Jb Models (p154–155). Celly School's second lesson of 7J Current electricity.
- **Size:** 139 spoken lines (Volty 128, Bolt 11), 24 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1460 words. That is roughly 17–18 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice Autonoe).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ia.

**World:** The Model Museum, beside the Bead Loop. Each circuit model stands in a glass case with a "Where it cracks" tag: Sam's water wheel, Nat's marble run, the central heating house, the coal train, and the rope loop.
- **Case:** "The case of the two models" (the 7Ja teaser).
- **Answered at the end:** Sam's water model is better. Its water, like current, is never lost, so the "current" is the same all round. Nat's marbles fall out, so his "current" is used up, and real current isn't.

**Main tricks:**
- "Models make predictions. Models have limits." Tap the M's.
- "Boiler is the battery. Radiator is the ray of light." The two key matches in the central heating model.
- "A good model behaves like the real thing."
