# 7Jd Changing the current — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Georg Ohm** (true story, Jd10): not in the book ◇. Ohm was a German schoolteacher who carefully measured how current depends on the push and the wire. He published his results in 1827, and many scientists in Germany were unimpressed at first. In 1841 Britain's Royal Society gave him the Copley Medal. The unit of resistance, the ohm, is named after him ◇. "Ohm's law" and the ohm are not in the Year 7 book, so the script gives the name as a story only, with no formula.
- ⚑ **Voltmeter "brick wall"** (Jd03_09): a voltmeter has a very high resistance (◇). The script says only that it goes across a component, never in the loop, which is the book's rule. Keep the brick-wall picture playful.
- ⚑ **Dimmer switch hook** (Jd00): real home dimmers mostly use electronics today (◇). The book teaches the variable resistor, so the script explains a dimmer as "a variable resistor, like the one in a dimmer". It doesn't claim every dimmer is a resistor.
- **Volty and voltage:** Volty's name was explained in 7Ja. Here he only says voltage is his "favourite word".
- **Book facts used:** voltage helps push the charges round; a higher voltage gives a bigger current; voltmeter, volts (V), connected across a component; voltage measures how much energy the current transfers; filaments make it harder for current to flow; resistance; resistor; variable resistor with a slider; more bulbs in series give a smaller current; in parallel, more branches give a bigger current and less overall resistance; the fairground and football match queues model; air is normally an insulator; lightning voltages up to 100 000 000 volts; robots, sensors, motors, a computer control system, and the driver flowchart (sees obstacle, decides to slow, presses brake).
- **Home lab:** a pencil dimmer with graphite, 2–3 AA cells, a small torch bulb. A grown-up does any cutting. The graphite can get warm. Never mains.
- ◇ **"Volts are the push. Amps are the flow."**, **"Ammeter along, voltmeter across"** and **"Resistance restricts"** are Volty-made tricks.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Jd Changing the current (p158–159) and 7Jd STEM: What do robot engineers do? (p160–161). Celly School's fourth lesson of 7J Current electricity.
- **Size:** 136 spoken lines (Volty 124, Bolt 12), 24 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1400 words. That is roughly 17–18 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice Autonoe).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ia.

**World:** The Bead Loop gets new controls. The Pusher (the cell) has a push dial for voltage. Squeeze Tunnels come in different widths (resistance). A Sliding Tunnel has a lever that makes it longer and tighter (the variable resistor). A new gadget, the Energy Scanner (the voltmeter), clips its two probes either side of a tunnel.
- **Case:** "The case of the dimmer switch" (the 7Jc teaser).
- **Answered at the end:** a variable resistor changes the resistance. More resistance means a smaller current, and the bulb glows dimmer without turning off. Less resistance makes it bright again.

**Main tricks:**
- "Volts are the push. Amps are the flow." Push both palms forward / roll hands round each other.
- "Ammeter along. Voltmeter across." A finger traces along the loop; two fingers make a bridge across.
- "Resistance restricts." Squeeze your hand into a narrow tube.
