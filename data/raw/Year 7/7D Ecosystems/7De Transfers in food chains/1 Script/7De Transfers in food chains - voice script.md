# 7De Transfers in food chains — voice script

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book p66–67 (7De Transfers in food chains: "How do energy and poisons move through food chains?"). Last lesson of unit 7D and of Year 7 biology. 151 lines, 14 scenes (hook + 13), 2 parts (Part 1: scenes 0–6, brain break, Part 2: scenes 7–13). About 1677 spoken words and 39 long pauses, roughly 20 minutes with pauses (scaled from 7Cd: 1592 words, 39 pauses, 19:22). Voice: Sulafat (Gemini Flash TTS). No sentence is longer than 15 words. The grown-ups workbook promo is the shared bumper (bump_promo1, bump_promo2, bump_bye), added at the join, as in 7Cd and 7Db.

Memory tricks: "Arrows point to the eater" (arrow = direction of energy flow, read as "is eaten by"), "Energy leaks at every link" (moving, keeping warm, waste), "Primary means first. Secondary means second." (tertiary means third), "Cide means kill" (pesticide), "Poison piles up at the top" (persistent poisons concentrate along the chain).

Hook: peregrine falcons disappearing in the UK in the 1970s; eggs broke before chicks hatched (SB Q9). Answer (scenes 9, 10, 13): DDT passed from worms to blackbirds to falcons and made their eggshells weak; shells broke when parents sat on them.

Recall from 7Dd (aligned with /home/claude/b7dd/script.txt): extinct (Easter Island palm), intra- vs inter-specific competition (two lynxes, same hare), and in scene 2 the food chain grass, hare, lynx with producer/consumer. 7Dd already taught producer, consumer, herbivore, carnivore, omnivore, predator, prey, top predator with syllable taps and "Each arrow points from the food to the animal that eats it", so 7De does not re-tap those words.

Teaser: hands over to chemistry 7Ea Mixtures and the new host Atomy ("a new lab and a new friend"; the river brown with mud, matching the 7Ea hook).

## Added or illustrative content (flagged)
- "Cide means kill" (scene 7): standard word origin (Latin -cida), not in the Student Book.
- "Aphids are tiny insects" (scene 5): general knowledge, not stated in the book text.
- One-dot-per-worm, ten worms per blackbird (scene 9): an illustrative model of diagram F, not book numbers. Celly says "Pretend".
- "Worms took in DDT" (scene 9) and "no chicks hatched" (scene 10) come from the Workbook answer, not the Student Book text.
- "Bioaccumulation" is not used: the book only says the poison "gets more concentrated further along the food chain", so the script says that.

- **De01_02** [calm, recalling] Last time, we found out how living things affect their habitats.
- **De01_03** [clear] On Easter Island, the palm tree became extinct. What does extinct mean?
*(long pause)*
- **De01_04** [pleased] There are none of that species left. It is gone for ever.
- **De01_05** [clear] Two lynxes chase the same hare. Is that intra-specific or inter-specific competition?
*(long pause)*

- **De01_07** [curious] Today, we follow a food chain closely. Two things travel along it. Energy, and poisons.
- **De01_08** [gentle] Wiggle your fingers. Where does the energy to move come from? Your food! Let's follow it.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 2
- **De02_01** [clear] Remember this food chain? Grass, then hare, then lynx.
- **De02_02** [curious] Which one is the producer?
*(long pause)*
- **De02_03** [pleased] The grass! It makes its own food, using light.
- **De02_04** [explaining] The hare and the lynx are consumers. They eat other organisms.
- **De02_05** [explaining] Living things store energy in the substances that make up their bodies.
- **De02_06** [explaining] When the hare eats grass, some energy passes from the grass to the hare.
- **De02_07** [clear] The arrows in a food chain show the direction that energy flows.
- **De02_08** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Arrows point to the eater. Follow the energy.
- **De02_09** [explaining] So you can read each arrow as, is eaten by. Grass is eaten by the hare.
- **De02_10** [curious] A rabbit eats a lettuce. Which way does the arrow point?
*(long pause)*
- **De02_11** [pleased] From the lettuce to the rabbit! The energy flows into the eater.
- **De02_12** [gentle] Point one arm like an arrow. Now point it at your mouth. Arrows point to the eater.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 3
- **De03_01** [clear] Here is another food chain. Lettuce, then rabbit, then fox.
- **De03_02** [curious] Does the fox get all the energy that was in the lettuce? Let's check.
- **De03_03** [explaining] Remember respiration? It releases energy from food.
- **De03_04** [clear] So why do organisms respire?
*(long pause)*
- **De03_05** [pleased] To release energy, so they can move.
- **De03_06** [explaining] The rabbit uses lots of energy to hop, run and keep warm.
- **De03_07** [explaining] A lot of energy stays in food it cannot digest. That passes out as waste.
- **De03_08** [explaining] Only some energy is stored in new substances in its body. Only that passes to the fox.
- **De03_09** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Energy leaks at every link. Moving, keeping warm, and waste.
- **De03_10** [curious] So why does the fox not get all the energy from the lettuces?
*(long pause)*
- **De03_11** [pleased] The rabbit used some to move and keep warm. And some was lost in its waste.
- **De03_12** [gentle] Jog on the spot for five seconds. That energy is used up. It will not pass on.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 4
- **De04_01** [explaining] Energy is lost at each stage. So there is less and less energy along the chain.
- **De04_02** [clear] That is why there are usually fewer organisms at each stage.
- **De04_03** [explaining] Hundreds of lettuces feed fewer rabbits. Those rabbits feed even fewer foxes.
- **De04_04** [explaining] We can show these numbers as a pyramid of numbers.
- **De04_05** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Pyr. A. Mid. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **De04_06** [explaining] Each bar shows the number at one stage. The widest bar is at the bottom.
- **De04_07** [clear] Each stage in a food chain is called a trophic level.
- **De04_08** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Tro. Phic. Two taps.
*(long pause)*
- **De04_09** [explaining] Producers are at the bottom. Next come primary consumers. They eat the producers.
- **De04_10** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Pri. Ma. Ry. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **De04_11** [explaining] Then come secondary consumers. They eat the primary consumers.
- **De04_12** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Sec. On. Da. Ry. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **De04_13** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Primary means first. Secondary means second.
- **De04_14** [gentle] Make a pyramid with your hands. Wide at the bottom. Pointy at the top.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 5
- **De05_01** [explaining] Here is a food chain from a field. Grass, grasshopper, frog, grass snake.

- **De05_07** [explaining] Now, a puzzle. Pyramids of numbers do not always look like pyramids.
