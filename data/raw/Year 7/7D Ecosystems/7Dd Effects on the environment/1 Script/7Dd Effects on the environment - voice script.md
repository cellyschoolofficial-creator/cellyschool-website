# 7Dd Effects on the environment — voice script

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book p62–63 (page numbers inferred from 7Db p58–59) (7Dd Effects on the environment: "How do organisms affect their habitats?"), plus the Greener cities STEM page (p64). 150 lines, 14 scenes (hook + 13), 2 parts (Part 1: scenes 1–6, brain break, Part 2: scenes 7–13). 40 long pauses. About 1680 spoken words, roughly 19–20 minutes with pauses (same size as 7Db: 146 lines, 1652 words). Voice: Sulafat (Gemini Flash TTS). No sentence is longer than 15 words. Memory tricks: "Silly Frogs Sing With My Owl" (animal resources: shelter, food, space, water, mates, oxygen), "Inter is like international" (inter-specific = between species, intra = inside one species), "Producers produce food. Consumers consume it", "No plants, no web". Hook and case: Easter Island (the 7Dc teaser already promises "an island that lost all its trees"). Scene 1 recall is aligned with the finished 7Dc script (Bilal's yellow cress, "Nocturnal and night both start with N", "Hibernate hides. Migrate moves."). Scene 12 is the home mission with a grown-up and a safety line. The grown-ups workbook promo is the shared bumper (bump_promo1, bump_promo2, bump_bye), added at the join, as in 7Db.

## Facts added beyond the book (please check)
- Each food-chain arrow points from the food to the eater (scene 5). Standard KS3 convention; 7De says the arrows show energy flow.
- Producers make food "using light" (scene 5). Book key only says "makes its own food"; photosynthesis needs light is in 7Dc.
- Omnivore definition, "eats both plants and animals" (scene 5). The book only lists the word.
- "A goshawk is a bird that hunts" (scene 4).
- "Silly Frogs Sing With My Owl" mnemonic is new (not Pearson).
- Food web C (scene 7) was read from a partly garbled scan plus the answer key: producers grass and aspen; hares, voles, moose, beetles eat plants; thrush eats beetles; goshawk eats thrushes, voles and hares; lynx, wolverine, wolf hunt; great horned owl eats goshawks. Please check the arrows against the printed page before drawing the web.

## Script

### Scene 0
- **Dd00_01** [curious, intriguing] Here is a mystery. An island has giant stone statues. But it has no trees at all.
- **Dd00_02** [curious] Long ago, the people there started to starve. Why?
- **Dd00_03** [warm] Today, we solve the case of the island with no trees.

### Scene 1
- **Dd01_01** [warm, smiling] Hello, scientist! Welcome back, detective.
- **Dd01_02** [calm, recalling] Last time, we saw how the environment affects living things. Like the oak dropping its leaves.
- **Dd01_03** [clear] Bilal grew cress seedlings in a dark cupboard. They turned yellow. What was the factor?
*(long pause)*
- **Dd01_04** [pleased] Light! There was not enough light. That is environmental variation.
- **Dd01_05** [clear] Owls hunt at night. What do we call animals that are active at night?
*(long pause)*
- **Dd01_06** [pleased] Nocturnal! Nocturnal and night both start with N.
- **Dd01_07** [clear] In winter, a hedgehog becomes inactive. It does not need food. What is this called?
*(long pause)*
- **Dd01_08** [pleased] Hibernation! Remember the trick. Hibernate hides. Migrate moves.
- **Dd01_09** [curious] Today, we flip it around. How do living things affect their environment?
- **Dd01_10** [gentle] Think of a tree near your home. Who uses it?
*(long pause)*

### Scene 2
- **Dd02_01** [clear] To survive and grow, living things need things from their habitat. We call these resources.
- **Dd02_02** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Re. Sour. Ces. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Dd02_03** [explaining] Animals need oxygen, food and water. They need shelter, space and mates.
- **Dd02_04** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Silly Frogs Sing With My Owl. Shelter, food, space, water, mates, oxygen.
- **Dd02_05** [explaining] Plants need light, air and water. They need warmth, space and mineral salts.
- **Dd02_06** [clear] All the members of one species, living in the same area, make a population.
- **Dd02_07** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Pop. U. La. Tion. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Dd02_08** [clear] If any resource is missing, the population goes down.
- **Dd02_09** [curious] Scientists are going into a desert. Name three resources they should take.
*(long pause)*
- **Dd02_10** [pleased] Water, food and shelter. A tent is shelter!
- **Dd02_11** [gentle] Breathe in slowly. That is oxygen, your first resource. Now breathe out.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 3
- **Dd03_01** [curious] Now, back to our mystery island. It is called Easter Island. It is in the Pacific Ocean.
- **Dd03_02** [explaining] A Dutch explorer named it. He saw huge stone statues, but no trees.
- **Dd03_03** [explaining] Scientists found clues. Long ago, the island was covered in trees.
- **Dd03_04** [explaining] Over hundreds of years, people cut the trees down. They used the wood for building, fires and fishing boats.
- **Dd03_05** [explaining] By about sixteen hundred, all the trees were gone.
- **Dd03_06** [explaining] With no wood, they could not build boats to fish. The people started to starve.
- **Dd03_07** [explaining] The birds disappeared too. They had no trees for shelter. And people ate them.
- **Dd03_08** [explaining] The Easter Island palm tree died out completely. It became extinct.
- **Dd03_09** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Ex. Tinct. Two taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Dd03_10** [clear] Extinct means there are none of that species left. It is gone for ever.
- **Dd03_11** [curious] Which organism made the Easter Island palm extinct?
*(long pause)*
- **Dd03_12** [pleased] Humans! The islanders cut down all the trees.
- **Dd03_13** [gentle] Stand tall like a palm tree. Now slowly sink down to the floor. Gone.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 4
- **Dd04_01** [clear] There are not enough resources for everyone. So organisms compete for them. This is competition.
- **Dd04_02** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Com. Pe. Ti. Tion. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Dd04_03** [explaining] Competition within one species is called intra-specific competition.
- **Dd04_04** [explaining] Competition between different species is called inter-specific competition.
- **Dd04_05** [explaining] A goshawk is a bird that hunts. Goshawks and lynxes both hunt hares.
- **Dd04_06** [explaining] So goshawks and lynxes are in inter-specific competition.
- **Dd04_07** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Inter is like international, between countries. Intra means inside one species.
- **Dd04_08** [explaining] Organisms with the best adaptations are more likely to survive and reproduce.
- **Dd04_09** [explaining] The others may move away, or die.
- **Dd04_10** [curious] Two lynxes chase the same hare. Is that intra or inter-specific competition?
*(long pause)*
- **Dd04_11** [pleased] Intra-specific! The two lynxes are the same species.
- **Dd04_12** [gentle] Reach out fast and grab a pretend apple, before anyone else does. Quick!
*(long pause)*

### Scene 5
- **Dd05_01** [clear] We can show what eats what with a food chain.
- **Dd05_02** [explaining] Here is one. Grass, then hare, then lynx.
- **Dd05_03** [explaining] Each arrow points from the food to the animal that eats it.
- **Dd05_04** [explaining] Grass makes its own food, using light. So it is a producer.
- **Dd05_05** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Pro. Du. Cer. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Dd05_06** [explaining] Animals cannot make their own food. They eat other organisms. They are consumers.
- **Dd05_07** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Con. Su. Mer. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Dd05_08** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Producers produce food. Consumers consume it. Consume means eat.
- **Dd05_09** [explaining] The hare eats plants, so it is a herbivore. The lynx eats animals, so it is a carnivore.
- **Dd05_10** [explaining] An animal that eats both plants and animals is an omnivore.
- **Dd05_11** [explaining] An animal that hunts other animals is a predator. The animal it hunts is its prey.
- **Dd05_12** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Pred. A. Tor. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Dd05_13** [explaining] A top predator is not prey for anything. Nothing hunts it.
- **Dd05_14** [curious] In grass, hare, lynx, which one is the producer?
*(long pause)*
- **Dd05_15** [pleased] The grass! It makes its own food.
- **Dd05_16** [gentle] Hook your fingers together like a chain. Link, link, link. Grass, hare, lynx.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 6
- **Dd06_01** [playful] Thumbs up if it is true. Thumbs down if it is false.
- **Dd06_02** [clear] Number one. Plants need mineral salts to grow well.
*(long pause)*
- **Dd06_03** [pleased] Thumbs up! Mineral salts are a plant resource.
- **Dd06_04** [clear] Number two. Two hares eating the same grass is inter-specific competition.
*(long pause)*
- **Dd06_05** [pleased] Thumbs down! They are the same species. So it is intra-specific.

*(long pause)*
- **Dd06_07** [pleased] Thumbs up! Producers produce food.

### Scene 7
- **Dd07_01** [warm] Welcome back, detective. Let's solve the case.
- **Dd07_02** [clear] Food chains join together to make a food web. Here is one from a cold forest in Canada.
- **Dd07_03** [explaining] Grass and aspen trees are the producers. Snowshoe hares, voles, moose and beetles eat plants.
- **Dd07_04** [explaining] Thrushes eat beetles. Goshawks eat thrushes, voles and hares.
- **Dd07_05** [explaining] Lynxes, wolverines and wolves hunt there too. The great horned owl even eats goshawks.
- **Dd07_06** [explaining] A food web shows how animals compete for the same food.
- **Dd07_07** [curious] Goshawks and lynxes both eat hares. What if the goshawks got a disease and died?
*(long pause)*
- **Dd07_08** [pleased] There would be more hares. So the lynx population may go up. They have more to eat.
- **Dd07_09** [clear] Here is the longest food chain. Aspen, beetle, thrush, goshawk, great horned owl.
- **Dd07_10** [explaining] The great horned owl is the top predator.
- **Dd07_11** [curious] Why are goshawks and wolverines in competition?
*(long pause)*
- **Dd07_12** [pleased] They both eat snowshoe hares.
- **Dd07_13** [gentle] Spread your fingers wide, like a web. Wiggle one finger. Your whole hand moves a little.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 8
- **Dd08_01** [curious] Detective thinking now. What if all the snowshoe hares died?
- **Dd08_02** [explaining] Hares and voles both eat grass. With no hares, there is more grass for voles. So there might be more voles.
- **Dd08_03** [explaining] But wait. The goshawks have no hares to eat. So they might eat more voles.
- **Dd08_04** [clear] So there might be fewer voles. Good detectives check every link.
- **Dd08_05** [curious] Now, what if there was no rain for a long time?
*(long pause)*
- **Dd08_06** [pleased] The plants would die. The voles would have less food. So there would be fewer voles.
- **Dd08_07** [encouraging] Here is a trick. No plants, no web. The whole food web depends on the producers.

### Scene 9
- **Dd09_01** [clear] Populations change, depending on how much food there is.
- **Dd09_02** [explaining] In northern Canada, the lynx is a predator. Its main prey is the snowshoe hare.
- **Dd09_03** [explaining] When there are lots of hares, lynxes have lots to eat. Their population goes up.
- **Dd09_04** [explaining] When there are fewer hares, the lynx population goes down. Some starve. Others move away.
- **Dd09_05** [explaining] A graph shows hares and lynxes from nineteen hundred to nineteen forty.
- **Dd09_06** [clear] Hares go up, then lynxes go up. Hares go down, then lynxes go down.
- **Dd09_07** [clear] Competition and predation affect other organisms. We call them biotic factors.
- **Dd09_08** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Bi. Ot. Ic. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Dd09_09** [explaining] Remember abiotic? It means not alive. Biotic factors come from living things.
- **Dd09_10** [curious] Name two biotic factors that could make the lynx population go down.
*(long pause)*
- **Dd09_11** [pleased] Fewer hares to eat. A disease. More predators. Or competition with another predator.
- **Dd09_12** [gentle] Lift one arm up high for the hares. Let the lynx arm follow. Now both slowly come down.
*(long pause)*

*(long pause)*
- **Dd10_12** [pleased] Different plants give different food and shelter. That suits different animals.
- **Dd10_13** [gentle] Hold one hand flat, like a branch. Rest your other hand on it, like a bird. Shelter!
*(long pause)*
