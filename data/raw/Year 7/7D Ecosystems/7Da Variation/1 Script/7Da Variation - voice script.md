Voice: Sulafat (Gemini Flash TTS). No sentence is longer than 15 words. Estimated length about 19 minutes with intro, break and promo (scaled from 7Cd: 1592 words, 39 pauses, 19:22; 7Da has 1574 words, 43 pauses).

Memory tricks: "Continuous Can be any value. Discontinuous Drops into groups." · "DRY MIX: Dependent, Responding, Y axis. Manipulated, Independent, X axis." · "Inter is between. Intra is inside one species." Recall from 7Ce: "Stimulants Speed. Depressants Drag."

Hook: the zoo animal (Ringerl) that never had babies with the olingos. Answer (scenes 8 and 13): Ringerl was an olinguito, a different species.

## Script

### Scene 0
- **Da00_01** [curious, intriguing] Here is a mystery. A small, furry animal lived in a zoo with some olingos.
- **Da00_02** [curious] The keepers waited for it to have babies with them. It never did. Why not?
- **Da00_03** [warm] Today, we solve the case of the animal hiding in plain sight.

### Scene 1
- **Da01_01** [warm, smiling] Hello, scientist! Welcome back, detective.
- **Da01_02** [calm, recalling] Last time, we found out how drugs change the way your body works.
- **Da01_03** [clear] What does a stimulant do to impulses?
*(long pause)*
- **Da01_04** [pleased] It speeds them up. Stimulants Speed.
- **Da01_05** [clear] And what does a depressant do?
*(long pause)*
- **Da01_06** [pleased] It slows them down. Depressants Drag.
- **Da01_07** [curious] Today, we start a brand new case. Ecosystems! Our first clue is variation.
- **Da01_08** [gentle] Look at your two hands. Are they exactly the same? Look closely.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 2
- **Da02_01** [explaining] Explorers travel to wild places to find out what lives there. They still find new things.
- **Da02_02** [explaining] In twenty thirteen, a new mammal was discovered in Ecuador. It is called the olinguito.
- **Da02_03** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] O. Lin. Gui. To. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Da02_04** [explaining] Some people live deep in the Amazon jungle. The Yanomami people live there.
- **Da02_05** [explaining] They hunt deer for food. The deer eat forest plants.
- **Da02_06** [clear] Can you make a food chain? Start with the forest plants.
*(long pause)*
- **Da02_07** [pleased] Forest plants, then deer, then people.
- **Da02_08** [curious] Apart from food, what else does an animal need from where it lives?
*(long pause)*
- **Da02_09** [pleased] Shelter, water, or a mate. Any of those is right.
- **Da02_10** [explaining] Exploring helps us find useful new things. But explorers can also damage the places they visit.
- **Da02_11** [gentle] Pretend you are an explorer. Hold up your binoculars and look around the room.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 3
- **Da03_01** [clear] The place where an organism lives is called its habitat.
- **Da03_02** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Hab. I. Tat. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Da03_03** [explaining] The Yanomami people live in a jungle habitat. A wolverine lives in an Arctic habitat.
- **Da03_04** [clear] What habitat do you live in?
*(long pause)*
- **Da03_05** [pleased] Maybe a town, a city, a village or the countryside. All good answers.
- **Da03_06** [clear] Can you name two habitats where you might find fish?
*(long pause)*
- **Da03_07** [pleased] A river, a lake, a pond, the sea. Well done!
- **Da03_08** [clear] The differences between living things are called variation.
- **Da03_09** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Var. I. A. Tion. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Da03_10** [explaining] A dolphin and a tuna both have fins. But only the dolphin has a blowhole. That is variation.

### Scene 4
- **Da04_01** [explaining] There is a lot of variation between different species. A lion looks very different from a zebra.
- **Da04_02** [clear] Variation between different species is called inter-specific variation.
- **Da04_03** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] In. Ter. Spe. Ci. Fic. Five taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Da04_04** [explaining] There is less variation within one species. Members of one species are quite alike.
- **Da04_05** [clear] Variation within one species is called intra-specific variation.
- **Da04_06** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] In. Tra. Spe. Ci. Fic. Five taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Da04_07** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Inter is between. Intra is inside one species.
- **Da04_08** [curious] A herd of zebras are all one species. So how do they vary?
*(long pause)*
- **Da04_09** [pleased] Their stripe patterns are all different. Some are bigger, too.
- **Da04_10** [explaining] All humans belong to one species. But we vary too.
- **Da04_11** [curious] Name two ways that people from different places can look different.
*(long pause)*
- **Da04_12** [pleased] Height, hair colour, skin colour, nose shape. Any two is great.

### Scene 5
- **Da05_01** [explaining] If you measure your class, very few people are exactly the same height.
- **Da05_02** [clear] Height is continuous variation. It can have any value, within limits.
- **Da05_03** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Con. Tin. U. Ous. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Da05_04** [explaining] Some people can roll their tongue. Others cannot. There is nothing in between.
- **Da05_05** [clear] Tongue rolling is discontinuous variation. It falls into set groups, called categories.
- **Da05_06** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Dis. Con. Tin. U. Ous. Five taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Da05_07** [explaining] Think about your feet. Your foot length, in centimetres, is continuous. It can be any number.
- **Da05_08** [explaining] But your shoe size is discontinuous. Shoes only come in set sizes.
- **Da05_09** [explaining] Blood group is discontinuous too. It is A, B, A B, or O.
- **Da05_10** [explaining] Having a scar, or having a cold, is discontinuous. You have one, or you do not.
- **Da05_11** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Say it with me. Continuous Can be any value. Discontinuous Drops into groups.
- **Da05_12** [curious] So how can you tell if variation is continuous?
*(long pause)*
- **Da05_13** [pleased] It can have any value in a range. Continuous Can be any value.
- **Da05_14** [gentle] Try to roll your tongue. Either answer is fine. That is discontinuous.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 6
- **Da06_01** [playful] Thumbs up if it is true. Thumbs down if it is false.
- **Da06_02** [clear] Number one. A habitat is the place where an organism lives.
*(long pause)*
- **Da06_03** [pleased] Thumbs up!
- **Da06_04** [clear] Number two. Shoe size is continuous variation.
*(long pause)*
- **Da06_05** [pleased] Thumbs down! Shoes come in set sizes, so it is discontinuous.
- **Da06_06** [clear] Number three. There is more variation between species than within a species.
*(long pause)*
- **Da06_07** [pleased] Thumbs up! Inter-specific variation is bigger.
- **Da06_08** [warm, proud] Brilliant detective work. Part one is done. Time for a break!

### Scene 7
- **Da07_01** [warm] Welcome back, detective. Let's crack the case.
- **Da07_02** [clear] A species is a group of organisms that can reproduce with one another.
- **Da07_03** [clear] And their offspring can reproduce too.
- **Da07_04** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Spe. Cies. Two taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Da07_05** [explaining] Two different species usually cannot reproduce together. Very rarely, they can.
- **Da07_06** [clear] Their offspring are called hybrids. Hybrids cannot reproduce.
- **Da07_07** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Hy. Brid. Two taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Da07_08** [explaining] A horse and a zebra can make a zebroid. A lion and a tiger can make a liger.
- **Da07_09** [curious] Ligers cannot reproduce. So what does that tell you about lions and tigers?
*(long pause)*
- **Da07_10** [pleased] Lions and tigers are different species.
- **Da07_11** [gentle] Make lion claws with your hands. Now make tiger claws. Roar softly!
*(long pause)*

### Scene 8
- **Da08_01** [curious] Now, back to our mystery animal in the zoo. Its name was Ringerl.
- **Da08_02** [explaining] Ringerl lived in a zoo in the nineteen seventies, with some olingos.
- **Da08_03** [explaining] Olingos and olinguitos look very alike. There is little variation between them.
- **Da08_04** [explaining] So everyone thought Ringerl was an olingo too.
- **Da08_05** [curious] Why did Ringerl never reproduce with the olingos?
*(long pause)*
- **Da08_06** [pleased] Ringerl was an olinguito! A different species from the olingos.
- **Da08_07** [clear] Only members of the same species can reproduce with one another.

### Scene 9
- **Da09_01** [explaining] When we measure something like height, we are measuring a variable.
- **Da09_02** [clear] A variable is anything that can change and be measured.
- **Da09_03** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Var. I. A. Ble. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Da09_04** [explaining] In an investigation, you choose the values of one variable. This is the independent variable.
- **Da09_05** [explaining] Then you measure another variable. This is the dependent variable. It depends on the first one.
- **Da09_06** [clear] The independent variable goes across, on the horizontal x axis.
- **Da09_07** [clear] The dependent variable goes up, on the vertical y axis.
- **Da09_08** [encouraging] Here is a trick. DRY MIX. Dependent, Responding, Y axis. Manipulated, Independent, X axis.
- **Da09_09** [curious] A class counts who can roll their tongue. Which variable did they choose?
*(long pause)*
- **Da09_10** [pleased] Tongue rolling! That is the independent variable. It goes on the x axis.
- **Da09_11** [explaining] They counted the number of students. That is the dependent variable, on the y axis.
- **Da09_12** [gentle] Sweep your arm across. That is the x axis. Now reach up high. The y axis!
*(long pause)*

### Scene 10
- **Da10_01** [explaining] Tongue rolling is discontinuous. So we draw a bar chart with a gap between each bar.
- **Da10_02** [explaining] The y axis shows a number of things counted. That is called a frequency.
- **Da10_03** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Fre. Quen. Cy. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Da10_04** [clear] So a bar chart of counts is also called a frequency diagram.
- **Da10_05** [explaining] Height is continuous. So first, we split the heights into groups.
- **Da10_06** [explaining] The groups must be the same size, and must not overlap.
- **Da10_07** [explaining] For leaf lengths, try twenty-six to thirty, then thirty-one to thirty-five, and so on.
- **Da10_08** [explaining] We count how many are in each group. Then we draw the bars with no gaps.
- **Da10_09** [explaining] Often the bars make a bell shape. Lots in the middle, and fewer at each end.
- **Da10_10** [clear] This bell shape is called a normal distribution.
- **Da10_11** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Dis. Tri. Bu. Tion. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Da10_12** [curious] A bar chart of eye colour. Gaps, or no gaps?
*(long pause)*
- **Da10_13** [pleased] Gaps! Eye colour is discontinuous.

### Scene 11
- **Da11_01** [explaining] Sometimes we want to know if two variables are linked. Then we draw a scatter graph.
- **Da11_02** [explaining] Each measurement is one small, neat cross on the graph.
- **Da11_03** [explaining] The wider the top of a tree, the bigger the mass of its leaves. That is a link.
- **Da11_04** [explaining] A line of best fit through the crosses makes the pattern easier to see.
- **Da11_05** [clear] About half the crosses are above the line. About half are below it.
- **Da11_06** [explaining] No pattern means no link. An oak tree's height and its leaf mass were not linked.
- **Da11_07** [explaining] Rose leaves show a link. The longer the leaf, the wider it is.
- **Da11_08** [curious] As people's height goes up, what happens to their foot length?
*(long pause)*
- **Da11_09** [pleased] Foot length goes up too! Taller people tend to have longer feet.
- **Da11_10** [explaining] Good graphs have a title, labelled axes with units, and evenly spaced numbers.
- **Da11_11** [gentle] Draw a small, neat cross in the air. Now draw a straight line through the middle.
*(long pause)*
