# 7Dc Effects of the environment — voice script

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book p60 (7Dc Effects of the environment: "How do environments affect organisms?"). 150 lines, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts (Part 1: scenes 0–6, brain break, Part 2: scenes 7–12). About 1552 spoken words and 47 long pauses, roughly 19 minutes with pauses (scaled from 7Cd: 1592 words, 39 pauses, 19:22). Voice: Sulafat (Gemini Flash TTS). No sentence is longer than 13 words. The grown-ups workbook promo is the shared bumper (bump_promo1, bump_promo2, bump_bye), added at the join, as in 7Db.

Hook: the case of the falling leaves. Why does an oak drop its leaves in autumn while a pine keeps them all winter? (Set up by the 7Db teaser: the sun, the moon and falling leaves.) Answer (scenes 7 and 12): the oak is deciduous; in winter there is little light for photosynthesis and its leaves lose water that cannot be replaced from frozen ground. The pine is evergreen; its tougher leaves do not lose much water.

Memory tricks: "From your parents? Inherited. From the world around you? Environmental." · "Nocturnal and night both start with N." · "Deciduous Drops. Evergreen is ever green." · "Hibernate hides. Migrate moves." Recall from 7Db: "Small ears save heat", "Spines save water", "Happy Cats Eat" (habitat, community, ecosystem).

### Scene 0 — Hook: the case of the falling leaves
- **Dc00_01** [curious, intriguing] Here is a mystery. Every autumn, an oak tree drops all of its leaves.
- **Dc00_02** [curious] But a pine tree nearby keeps its leaves, all winter long.
- **Dc00_03** [curious] Why does the oak let go, while the pine holds on?
- **Dc00_04** [warm] Today, we solve the case of the falling leaves.

*(long pause)*

- **Dc01_04** [clear] Light, wind and temperature make the environment. Are they alive?
*(long pause)*
- **Dc01_05** [pleased] No! They are physical factors. We call them abiotic.
- **Dc01_06** [clear] Why does a polar bear have small ears?

*(long pause)*

- **Dc02_08** [curious] A scar, an earring, and eye colour. Which one is inherited?

### Scene 3 — Plants and environmental factors

- **Dc03_05** [curious] Bilal grew cress seedlings in a dark cupboard. They turned yellow. What was the factor?

- **Dc03_07** [curious] After a storm, an apple tree had very few leaves. What was the factor?

*(long pause)*
- **Dc03_10** [pleased] The fertiliser!
- **Dc03_11** [explaining] Now meet the hydrangea. We say hy-drain-ja. Its flowers change colour with the soil.

### Scene 7 — Deciduous and evergreen trees

- **Dc07_02** [curious] Back to our trees. Winter is a hard time for plants.

- **Dc07_04** [explaining] The ground can freeze. Then the roots cannot take up water.
- **Dc07_05** [explaining] Leaves lose water all the time. In winter, that water cannot be replaced.
- **Dc07_06** [clear] So some trees drop all their leaves in winter. They are deciduous trees.
- **Dc07_07** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] De. Cid. U. Ous. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Dc07_08** [explaining] We say di-sid-you-us. The oak tree is deciduous.
- **Dc07_09** [explaining] Other trees keep their leaves all year round. They are evergreen trees.
- **Dc07_10** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Ev. Er. Green. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Dc07_11** [explaining] Evergreen trees have tougher leaves. They do not lose much water.
- **Dc07_12** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Deciduous Drops. Evergreen is ever green.
- **Dc07_13** [curious] Why does an oak tree lose its leaves in winter?
*(long pause)*
- **Dc07_14** [pleased] So it does not lose water, when the ground is frozen.
- **Dc07_15** [gentle] Hold your arms out like branches. Wiggle your fingers. Let the leaves fall.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 8 — Plants in winter

- **Dc08_05** [curious] Why might a poppy die in winter?
*(long pause)*
- **Dc08_06** [pleased] It does not need to make food when there is little light.
- **Dc08_07** [explaining] Far in the north, winter has almost no daylight. Plants there drop their leaves, or wait as bulbs or seeds.
