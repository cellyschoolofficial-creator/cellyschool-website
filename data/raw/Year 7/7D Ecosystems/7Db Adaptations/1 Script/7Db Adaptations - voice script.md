# 7Db Adaptations — voice script

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book p58–59 (Adaptations). 146 lines, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts (Part 1: scenes 1–6, brain break, Part 2: scenes 7–12). About 1651 spoken words, roughly 19–20 minutes with pauses. Voice: Sulafat (Gemini Flash TTS). No sentence is longer than 20 words. Memory tricks: "Small ears save heat", "Spines save water", "Happy Cats Eat" (habitat, community, ecosystem). The grown-ups workbook promo is the shared bumper (bump_promo1, bump_promo2, bump_bye), added at the join, as in 7Cd.

## Script

### Scene 0
- **Db00_01** [curious, intriguing] Here is a mystery. A rabbit lives in a hot, dry desert. It never drinks water.
- **Db00_02** [curious] So how does it stay alive?
- **Db00_03** [warm] Today, we solve the case of the rabbit that never drinks.

### Scene 1
- **Db01_01** [warm, smiling] Hello, scientist! Welcome back, detective.
- **Db01_02** [calm, recalling] Last time, we explored variation. Variation means the differences between living things.
- **Db01_03** [clear] Height can be any value. Is that continuous or discontinuous variation?
*(long pause)*
- **Db01_04** [pleased] Continuous! It can have any value.
- **Db01_05** [clear] Shoe sizes come in set sizes. Continuous or discontinuous?
*(long pause)*
- **Db01_06** [pleased] Discontinuous! It falls into set groups.
- **Db01_07** [clear] And what is a species?
*(long pause)*
- **Db01_08** [pleased] A group that can reproduce together, to make young that can also reproduce.
- **Db01_09** [curious] Today, we ask a big question. Why are living things so different from each other?
- **Db01_10** [gentle] Look at your hand. Now imagine a bear's paw. Why so different? Let's find out.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 2
- **Db02_01** [clear] A habitat is the place where an organism lives. A desert, a pond, or the Arctic.
- **Db02_02** [explaining] The conditions in a habitat are its environment.
- **Db02_03** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] En. Vi. Ron. Ment. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Db02_04** [explaining] Physical factors make these conditions. How much light. How wet it is. How windy. The temperature.
- **Db02_05** [explaining] Soil and how high up you are can matter too.
- **Db02_06** [explaining] These factors are not alive. So we call them abiotic factors.
- **Db02_07** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] A. Bi. Ot. Ic. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Db02_08** [explaining] Here is a word clue. Bio means life. A at the start means not. Abiotic means not alive.
- **Db02_09** [curious] Ant, light, frog, wind. Which two are physical factors?
*(long pause)*
- **Db02_10** [pleased] Light and wind! They are not alive.
- **Db02_11** [explaining] A desert is dry, and hot in the day. The Arctic is cold and snowy.
- **Db02_12** [explaining] A grassy savanna is sunny and hot. A seashore is salty and windy.
- **Db02_13** [gentle] Look around your room. Is it light or dark? Warm or cool? That is your environment.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 3
- **Db03_01** [explaining] Living things have features that help them survive in their environment.
- **Db03_02** [clear] We say they are adapted to their habitat.
- **Db03_03** [clear] A feature that helps an organism survive is called an adaptation.
- **Db03_04** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Ad. Ap. Ta. Tion. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Db03_05** [explaining] Think of a fish. It has gills to get oxygen from the water.
- **Db03_06** [explaining] It has fins to swim. Its smooth, streamlined shape helps it move easily through water.
- **Db03_07** [explaining] But fins cannot walk on land. And gills cannot breathe air.
- **Db03_08** [clear] So a fish is adapted to water, but not to land.
- **Db03_09** [curious] Why can a fish not live on land?
*(long pause)*
- **Db03_10** [pleased] Its gills cannot breathe air, and its fins cannot walk.
- **Db03_11** [gentle] Put your hands together and swim them like a fish. Swish, swish.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 4
- **Db04_01** [curious] Now let's visit the Arctic. Meet the polar bear.
- **Db04_02** [explaining] It has white fur for camouflage. It can creep up on seals without being seen.
- **Db04_03** [explaining] It has thick fur to help keep it warm.
- **Db04_04** [explaining] It has small ears, so it does not lose too much heat.
- **Db04_05** [explaining] It has rough soles on its feet, to grip the ice.
- **Db04_06** [explaining] Its large feet spread out its weight, so it does not sink in the snow. They help it swim too.
- **Db04_07** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Say it with me. Small ears save heat.
- **Db04_08** [curious] How is a polar bear adapted to the cold?
*(long pause)*
- **Db04_09** [pleased] Thick fur keeps it warm. Small ears save heat.
- **Db04_10** [explaining] The Arctic hare has white fur too. Animals that want to eat it find it hard to spot.
- **Db04_11** [gentle] Spread your fingers wide, like a big bear foot. Press down gently on the table.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 5
- **Db05_01** [curious] Now let's visit a hot, dry desert. Meet the saguaro cactus.
- **Db05_02** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Sa. Gua. Ro. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Db05_03** [explaining] We say suh-wah-roh. Its roots spread over a large area. They soak up as much rain as possible.
- **Db05_04** [playful] After a storm, it can soak up a tonne of water in one day!
- **Db05_05** [explaining] Its thick stem stores the water.
- **Db05_06** [explaining] It has spines, not leaves. So it loses less water than a plant with leaves.
- **Db05_07** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Spines save water.
- **Db05_08** [curious] Why do cacti have spines instead of leaves?
*(long pause)*
- **Db05_09** [pleased] To reduce water loss. Spines save water.
- **Db05_10** [explaining] Now meet the jack rabbit. It lives in the desert too.
- **Db05_11** [explaining] Its large ears let heat escape, so it does not get too hot. They help it hear well too.
- **Db05_12** [explaining] Its large back legs let it run away fast, at up to seventy kilometres an hour.
- **Db05_13** [explaining] A meerkat in the desert has short hair, to lose heat.
- **Db05_14** [explaining] A wolverine in the cold has thick hair, to keep heat in.
- **Db05_15** [explaining] Some adaptations are things an animal does. This is called behaviour.
- **Db05_16** [playful] Desert vultures wee on their own legs! The wee dries up, and that cools them down.
- **Db05_17** [gentle] Cup your hands behind your ears, like big rabbit ears. Listen. Can you hear more?
*(long pause)*

### Scene 6
- **Db06_01** [playful] Thumbs up if it is true. Thumbs down if it is false.
- **Db06_02** [clear] Number one. Temperature is a physical environmental factor.
*(long pause)*
- **Db06_03** [pleased] Thumbs up! It is not alive. It is abiotic.
- **Db06_04** [clear] Number two. A polar bear has big ears to keep warm.
*(long pause)*
- **Db06_05** [pleased] Thumbs down! Small ears save heat.
- **Db06_06** [clear] Number three. A cactus has spines so it loses less water.
*(long pause)*
- **Db06_07** [pleased] Thumbs up! Spines save water.
- **Db06_08** [warm, proud] Brilliant detective work. Part one is done. Time for a break!

### Scene 7
- **Db07_01** [warm] Welcome back, detective. Let's crack the case.
- **Db07_02** [clear] All the plants and animals in a habitat make up a community.
- **Db07_03** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Com. Mu. Ni. Ty. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Db07_04** [explaining] Members of a community often have similar adaptations. They face the same problems.
- **Db07_05** [explaining] Fast rivers can sweep things away. So many river animals have suckers to hold on to rocks.
- **Db07_06** [explaining] The hogsucker fish has a sucker to grip rocks. Its body is bendy and streamlined.
- **Db07_07** [curious] Imagine a plant in that fast river. How could it stay safe?
*(long pause)*
- **Db07_08** [pleased] A bendy stem lets water flow over it. Strong roots hold it firmly in the ground.
- **Db07_09** [explaining] Pond plants are adapted too. Long leaf stems let their leaves float on top of the water.
- **Db07_10** [explaining] Their roots grow in the soil at the bottom of the pond, to get nutrients.
- **Db07_11** [gentle] Stand up and sway like a bendy river plant. Keep your feet stuck down like roots.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 8
- **Db08_01** [clear] Now the biggest word today. The community, plus all the physical factors, make an ecosystem.
- **Db08_02** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] E. Co. Sys. Tem. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Db08_03** [explaining] The Arctic ecosystem is cold and snowy. Polar bears, wolverines and pine trees live there.
- **Db08_04** [clear] So, which is bigger? A community, or an ecosystem?
*(long pause)*
- **Db08_05** [pleased] An ecosystem! It is the community plus the physical factors.
- **Db08_06** [encouraging] Here is a trick for the order. Happy Cats Eat. Habitat, then community, then ecosystem.
- **Db08_07** [explaining] Habitat is the place. Community is the living things. Ecosystem is everything together.
- **Db08_08** [gentle] Make a small circle with your hands. Habitat. Bigger. Community. Biggest. Ecosystem!
*(long pause)*

### Scene 9
- **Db09_01** [curious] Here is another clue. Let's find out where adaptations come from.
- **Db09_02** [explaining] Many features come from your parents. We say they are inherited.
- **Db09_03** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] In. Her. It. Ed. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Db09_04** [explaining] A polar bear got its white fur from its parents. It did not choose it.
- **Db09_05** [explaining] Instructions for features are carried on genes. Genes are inside the nucleus of a cell.
- **Db09_06** [explaining] You know about gametes. Each parent gives one gamete, an egg cell or a sperm cell.
- **Db09_07** [explaining] Each gamete has a slightly different mix of instructions.
- **Db09_08** [clear] So each child inherits a slightly different mix of features. That is why brothers and sisters differ.
- **Db09_09** [explaining] Identical twins are the exception. They both grow from the same fertilised egg cell, the zygote.
- **Db09_10** [explaining] Polar bears and meerkats differ a lot. Two polar bears differ only a little.
- **Db09_11** [curious] Kim's mother has blue eyes. Her father has brown eyes. Kim has blue eyes. Who did they come from?
*(long pause)*
- **Db09_12** [pleased] Her mother!
- **Db09_13** [curious] Kim's brother Harry has brown eyes. Why are they a bit different?
*(long pause)*
- **Db09_14** [pleased] They came from different gametes. Each gamete has a slightly different mix of instructions.
- **Db09_15** [gentle] Touch three features you inherited. Your hair. Your nose. Your ears.
*(long pause)*

- **Db10_07** [explaining] Hares are adapted too. A brown farmland hare has brown fur, so it is hard to spot.
- **Db10_08** [explaining] An Arctic hare has thick white fur, small ears and very wide feet for snow.
- **Db10_09** [explaining] Deep in the ocean, it is very cold and very dark.

*(long pause)*
