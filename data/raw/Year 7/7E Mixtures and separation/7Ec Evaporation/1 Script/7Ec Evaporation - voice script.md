# 7Ec Evaporation — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ Gandhi's Salt March: book-adjacent (Activity Pack 7Ec-8 "Gandhi and the Salt Act"). Dates 12 March to 6 April 1930 (24 days); distance about 385–390 km, sources vary, so the script says "more than three hundred and eighty kilometres". The hook says "that handful shook an empire": a fair summary, not a quote. "Mahatma" is a title of respect; kept because it is how he is known.
- ⚑ "If it boils at one hundred degrees, it is pure water" follows the Pearson answer (SB 7Ec Q6). Strictly, 100 °C is at normal air pressure; not mentioned to keep it simple.
- ◇ "The bubbles are water turned into gas" (Bolt's air-bubble misconception) is a standard KS3 addition; the book only says "bubbles of gas newly made from the liquid", which matches.
- ◇ Stalactites "slowly grow" (book: each drop leaves a tiny amount of solid). No timescale given on purpose.
- Home demo uses warm tap water poured by the grown-up (same rule as 7Eb: only a grown-up uses warm water). No flame, no tasting. Table salt crystals are cube-shaped; the script only asks "what shape?".
- Bunsen burner is taught as knowledge only and the script says twice that it is for the school lab with a teacher.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Ec Safety when heating (Working Scientifically, p76–77) and 7Ec Evaporation (p78–79), with Workbook 7Ec and Activity Pack 7Ec-1, 7Ec-2, 7Ec-6 to 7Ec-9. Celly School's third chemistry lesson. 165 spoken lines (Atomy 146, Bolt 19), 37 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1990 words, roughly 21–22 minutes with pauses. Host: **Atomy the atom** (voice Zephyr). Sidekick: **Bolt** the robot (his own small, bright robot voice). Sentences are 15 words or fewer.
Lesson grammar (same as 7Ea and 7Eb): Atomy asks a prediction, Bolt gives a confident wrong idea, the demo tests it on screen, the Zoom Lens shows why at particle level, then a memory trick or gesture, then quick retrieval. Bolt is never mocked; he reboots happily and gets the true-or-false right by the end.
World: Atomy's Unmixing Lab. Case: "The case of the hidden salt" (the 7Eb teaser: "Next time, we turn up the heat. How do we get the salt back out of salty water?"). Hook: Gandhi's handful of salty mud, 1930. Evidence tool: an evaporating basin. Answered at the end: the salt was dissolved, so the filter could not catch it; evaporation sends the water into the air and leaves the salt behind.
Main tricks: "Evaporation at the Edge. Boiling Bubbles in the Body." (fingertips flutter off a flat hand; fists bubble up). "Yellow is for Yawning. Blue is for Busy." (yawn; rub hands). "Close. Light. Gas." (tap three fingers). "The solvent goes. The solute stays."
