# 7Ed Chromatography — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ◇ **Solvent front** is not in the Student Book or glossary. It was asked for, and it is standard KS3, so it gets one line and a tap line (scene 4). Cut Ed04_09 and Ed04_10 if you want to stay strictly in the book.
- ⚑ "Black ink splits into red, purple and blue" and "red is carried fastest" follow the Pearson answers to SB Q4a/b (black ink contains blue, purple and red; red was carried fastest). Real pens vary, so the animation should match the script, not a real pen.
- ⚑ The three-pens case (scene 7) and the three-drinks colours (scene 8) are invented for the game. Only "drink 3 has no tartrazine" is the book's answer (SB Q7b). Scene 8 says tartrazine "is thought to make some children over-active", the book's own wording.
- ⚑ "Chroma means colour, graphy means writing, gram means picture": chroma and graph are right; -gram is really "something written or drawn", so "picture" is a simplification.
- ⚑ Mikhail Tsvet: Russian botanist (born in Italy), first talk 1903, key papers 1906; chalk (calcium carbonate) column; "tsvet" (цвет) means colour in Russian. Not in the Student Book. The script says "over one hundred years ago" and does not give a date.
- ◇ "Dab, dry, dab again" for a concentrated dot comes from AP 7Ed-2 Q1.
- Home demo: water only, washable felt-tips, no nail varnish remover or other solvents (Bolt asks, Atomy says no).

Host: **Atomy the atom** (voice Zephyr). Sidekick: **Bolt** the robot (his own small, bright robot voice). Sentences are 15 words or fewer.
Lesson grammar (same as 7Ea–7Ec): Atomy asks a prediction, Bolt gives a confident wrong idea, the demo tests it on screen, the Zoom Lens or close-up shows why, then a memory trick or gesture, then quick retrieval. Bolt is never mocked; he reboots happily and gets the true-or-false right by the end.
World: Atomy's Unmixing Lab. Case: "The case of the three black pens" (the 7Ec teaser: "Next time, a secret note is written in black ink. Three black pens look the same. Which one wrote it?"). Evidence tool: a strip of chromatography paper and a jar of water. Answered at the end: the note's chromatogram (blue, purple, yellow at the same heights) matches pen B.
Main tricks: "Pencil stays Put. Ink Itches to run." (tap the desk; wiggle fingers up). "Dots Above, Dip Below." (one flat hand above the other). "Count the spots, count the dyes." Word parts: chroma = colour, graphy = writing, gram = picture. "Black is a team of colours in disguise."
