# 7Ee Distillation — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ◇ **Distillate** is not in the Student Book or glossary (the book says "pure (distilled) water"). It was asked for, so it gets one line and a tap line (scene 5). Cut Ee05_14 and Ee05_15 if you want to stay strictly in the book.
- **Fractional distillation is not in the Pearson Student Book**, so it is left out. Ethanol's boiling point (78 °C) is used only as a boiling-point "name tag" in the scene 7 game. (Activity Pack 7Ee-6 Q5 touches on ethanol and water; left for paper.)
- ⚑ Jebel Ali (UAE) "over 800 million litres a day" is the book's figure. The plant uses distillation and reverse osmosis, so the script says "one way is called distillation", not that Jebel Ali only distils.
- ⚑ "Salty water boils above one hundred degrees" comes from the Workbook 7Ee answer. The game's 102 °C for liquid A is an invented reading for a very salty sample (real sea water boils at about 100.5 °C; strongly salty water is higher). The pure-water test assumes normal air pressure.
- ⚑ Charles Wilson, 1872, solar still for a mining community in Chile: the book's facts. His nationality and job are left out because sources disagree.
- ⚑ Distillation in the Arabic world: Al-Kindi (9th century, Baghdad) wrote a book on perfume that describes distillation (e.g. rose water); "alembic" comes from Arabic al-anbiq. Distillation is older (Alexandria), so the script says "used and improved… and wrote it down", not "invented". Jabir ibn Hayyan is left out on purpose. Not in the Student Book.
- ◇ "Boil it, Cool it, Catch it": the book says the water evaporates when heated; in the lab flask it boils (hence anti-bumping granules), so "boil" is fine for the lab still. For the solar still the script says "evaporates", not "boils".
- The book's LIFESAVER® filter jerrycan (Safe drinking water page) is not named in the script, to avoid a brand name; the "one in eight people" fact is used in scene 10.
- Home demo: no heating, no boiling, never drink it; test by drying a drop.
