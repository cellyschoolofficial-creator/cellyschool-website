# 7Kc Friction — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Egyptian statue hook and true story** (Kc00, Kc10): ◇ not in the book. A wall painting in the tomb of Djehutihotep in Egypt, about 3900 years old, shows a huge statue being pulled on a wooden sledge. One worker pours liquid onto the sand in front of it. In 2014, physicists at the University of Amsterdam tested sledges on sand. With the right amount of water, the sand got stiffer and the pull needed was much smaller (reported as roughly half). The script says "much less pulling force", gives no figure, and says "scientists think" for why the water was poured. Some experts once thought it was part of a ceremony.
- ⚑ **Brakes in the rain** (Kc05): the book asks why bicycle brakes don't work well in the rain, but doesn't give the answer. The script says the water acts a bit like a lubricant, so there is less friction (◇ standard answer).
- ⚑ **Smooth glass has tiny bumps** (Kc09): ◇, a standard picture, and a simplification. Friction isn't only bumps catching. The script shows it as a model with a crack.
- **Book facts used:** friction is the force between two touching objects; it can slow things down or make things stay still; clothes on a chair; walking; rubber makes a lot of friction; racing-car tyres of special rubber; abseiling uses friction to control speed; rubber bath mats; skiers wax skis; smooth surfaces and lubricants (oil, grease), lubrication; oil the axles, never the brake blocks; tyre tread lets water escape; friction wears things away (brake pads, tyres, clothes); friction makes heat and noise; an engine with no oil overheats; rusty hinges squeak; air and water resistance are reduced by smooth surfaces and shapes; the drag racer's parachutes increase air resistance.
- **Safety (home lab):** paperback exercise books only; for the rice bottle, a plastic bottle and an unsharpened pencil or a wooden spoon handle; keep faces back in case it slips; clean up spilled rice.
- ◇ **"Friction fights the movement"**, **"Oil the axles, never the brakes"** and **"Grip, grind, glow"** are Volty-made tricks.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Kc Friction (p170–171). Celly School's third lesson of 7K Forces.
- **Size:** 132 spoken lines (Volty 121, Bolt 11), 23 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1340 words. That is roughly 16–17 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice Autonoe).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ia.

**World:** The Ice Rink and Climbing Wall in the Arrow Arena. Specs on: wherever two surfaces touch, tiny cartoon grip bumps appear and catch on each other, and a red friction arrow points against the movement. Skiers wax their skis and the bumps smooth out. An abseiler slides down the climbing wall, using rope friction to control her speed. Newt the referee watches from his high chair.
- **Case:** "The case of the water-pouring worker" (the 7Kb teaser).
- **Answered at the end:** the worker wasn't lazy. Pouring the right amount of water on the sand made it firmer. That lowered the friction under the sledge, so the team needed much less pulling force.

**Main tricks:**
- "Friction fights the movement." One palm slides; the other palm pushes back against it.
- "Oil the axles, never the brakes." Spin a finger round for the axle, then a flat hand stop sign for the brakes.
- "Grip, grind, glow." Friction gives grip, grinds things away, and makes things warm. Rub your hands for glow.
