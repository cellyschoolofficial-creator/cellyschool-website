# 7Ke Balanced and unbalanced — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Tug-of-war at the Olympics** (true story, Ke10): ◇ not in the book. Tug-of-war was an Olympic sport from 1900 to 1920. At the 1908 London Olympics, British police teams won all three medals; the City of London Police won gold. The City of London Police won gold again in 1920. Well documented. The script keeps to these facts.
- ⚑ **A disadvantage of a very stretchy rope** (Ke08, book Q1b): the book asks, but gives no answer. The script says the climber would fall further before stopping (◇ standard answer).
- ⚑ **Dogs get tired** (Ke02, book Q3): the book asks what happens. The script says the pulling force gets smaller, the forces are unbalanced, and the sled slows down (◇ standard answer, using the book's idea that friction balances the dogs at a steady speed).
- **Unit finale** (scene 12): a short recap of 7Ka–7Ke, then the 7L Sound teaser (the 7La hook from the physics design doc: a hummingbird, a gorilla, a grasshopper and you all make sounds the same way).
- **Book numbers:** the paper bridge spans a 50 cm gap and holds a 1 kg mass. The ice-cream-stick bridge that held 909 kg (SB p179) is left out of the script (the OCR is unclear).
- **Book facts used:** forces add together (pushing a car, the dog team); balanced forces are the same size in opposite directions, and nothing changes; the mule and the man; unbalanced forces start movement or change speed; the three stages of the bike ride; all stationary objects have balanced forces; moving objects with balanced forces keep the same speed; the force meter's spring pulls back until the forces balance; the chair pushes back like a spring; the tightrope stretches and pulls back; measuring friction at a steady speed; architects and civil engineers; conventions; the paper bridge; climbing ropes, safety standards, and a stretchier rope giving a smaller impact force.
- **Safety (home lab):** gentle towel pulls on a soft, clear floor, no sudden letting go. Keep the sugar bag low over a table.
- ◇ **"Balanced: boringly the same"**, **"Unbalanced: something changes"** and **"Zero on the scoreboard, no change"** are Volty-made tricks.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Ke Balanced and unbalanced (p176–177), 7Ke STEM: Structures (p178–179) and 7Ke Sports safety (p180). Celly School's fifth and last lesson of 7K Forces.
- **Size:** 132 spoken lines (Volty 120, Bolt 12), 22 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1360 words. That is roughly 16–17 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice Autonoe).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ia.

**World:** The Tug-of-War Final in the Arrow Arena. Newt the referee's Net Force Scoreboard moves to centre stage. It adds up all the arrows on one object. Score zero means balanced: nothing changes. Any other score means unbalanced: the speed changes. A mule and a man pull a rope. A dog-sled team pulls together. A cyclist rides round the track.
- **Case:** "The case of the cyclist who couldn't speed up" (the 7Kd teaser).
- **Answered at the end:** her forward force and the backward forces (friction and air resistance) are equal. The forces are balanced, so nothing changes: she keeps going at the same steady speed. A still rock has balanced forces too.

**Main tricks:**
- "Balanced: boringly the same." A flat hand gliding smoothly. Still stays still; steady stays steady.
- "Unbalanced: something changes." Push forward to speed up, pull back to slow down.
- "Zero on the scoreboard, no change." Make a zero with your fingers.

### Scene 2 · Forces add up
- **Ke02_01** [clear] Forces can add together.
- **Ke02_02** [warm] One person pushing a broken-down car finds it very hard.
- **Ke02_03** [warm] If a friend helps, it is easier. Their forces add together.
- **Ke02_04** [warm] Look at this dog sled. The pulling forces of all the dogs add up.
- **Ke02_05** [warm] On the scoreboard, their little arrows join into one giant arrow.
- **Ke02_06** [curious] The dogs get tired. They can't pull as strongly. What happens to the sled?
*(long pause)*
- **Ke02_07** [pleased] It slows down. Friction is now bigger than the dogs' pull.
- **Ke02_08 BOLT** [BOLT: proud] Teamwork makes bigger arrows. Go, dogs, go!

### Scene 3 · Balanced forces
- **Ke03_01** [warm] Now a man and a mule pull on a rope, in opposite directions.
- **Ke03_02** [warm] The rope is not moving. Their pulling forces are equal and opposite.
- **Ke03_03** [clear] When two forces are the same size, but in opposite directions, they are balanced.
- **Ke03_04** [slow] Bal. Anced. Two taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Ke03_05** [warm] Balanced forces mean nothing changes. Newt's scoreboard says zero.
- **Ke03_06** [playful] Here is a trick. Zero on the scoreboard, no change.
- **Ke03_07** [gentle] Make a zero with your fingers. Zero. No change!
*(long pause)*
- **Ke03_08** [curious] What will happen if the mule pulls harder?
*(long pause)*
- **Ke03_09** [pleased] The rope moves towards the mule! The forces are no longer balanced.

### Scene 4 · Unbalanced forces
- **Ke04_01** [clear] If one force is bigger than the other, the forces are unbalanced.
- **Ke04_02** [slow] Un. Bal. Anced. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Ke04_03** [warm] Unbalanced forces make something still start to move.
- **Ke04_04** [warm] If the object is already moving, unbalanced forces change its speed.
- **Ke04_05** [warm] Bigger force forwards, it speeds up. Bigger force backwards, it slows down.
- **Ke04_06** [playful] Here is a trick. Balanced: boringly the same. Unbalanced: something changes.
- **Ke04_07** [gentle] Glide one flat hand smoothly. Boringly the same. Now push it forward fast. Change!
*(long pause)*
- **Ke04_08 BOLT** [BOLT: thinking] So the scoreboard is never zero when something speeds up.
- **Ke04_09** [pleased] Exactly, Bolt. Speeding up means unbalanced forces.

### Scene 5 · The bike ride (Bolt: steady speed needs a bigger push)
- **Ke05_01** [warm] Let's follow a girl on her bike, in three stages.
- **Ke05_02** [warm] Stage one. Her forward force is bigger than the backward force. She speeds up.
- **Ke05_03** [warm] Stage two. The forces are equal. They are balanced.
- **Ke05_04 BOLT** [BOLT: very sure] Balanced means stopped. So she must stop!
- **Ke05_05** [gentle] No, Bolt. She is already moving. So she keeps moving, at the same speed.
- **Ke05_06** [warm] Stage three. She stops pedalling. There is no forward force.
- **Ke05_07** [warm] Friction and air resistance still pull back. She slows down, until she stops.
- **Ke05_08** [curious] How could she make the friction force on her bike bigger?
*(long pause)*
- **Ke05_09** [pleased] Use the brakes! A bigger friction force slows her down faster.
