# 7Ka Different forces — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Lunar buggy mass** (Ka07): the book's photo F labels the buggy "mass = 210 kg" (read from a scanned page). The script uses 210 kg and about 2100 N on Earth (book rule: about 10 N for every kilogram). It never gives a number for the Moon, because the book doesn't. Check the label against the printed book before recording.
- ⚑ **"Le Grand K"** (true story, Ka10): the book says only that from 1889 until 2019 all other kilogram masses were measured against one mass in Paris, and that the kilogram is now defined using a special balance. The nickname "Le Grand K", the three glass covers, and the tiny drift between copies are ◇, beyond the book. The script says "tiny amounts" and gives no figure.
- ⚑ **"Push, pull or twist"**: the book's text says "a push or a pull". The design doc quotes the glossary as "a push, pull or twist". The script says "push or pull" and mentions a twist once as a kind of push and pull together (Ka02_02). Check the glossary wording.
- **Astronaut hook:** this is the 7Je teaser. The answer stays in the book: the same mass, but the Moon's gravity is weaker, so the weight is smaller. No ◇ "one sixth" figure.
- **Book facts used:** a force can change shape, speed or direction; contact forces (friction, air resistance, water resistance, upthrust "the force that makes things float"); non-contact forces (gravity, static electricity, magnetism, which attracts iron and some other metals and can repel magnets); a man climbing a ship's side with magnets; newtons (N); arrows show direction and size; weight is the force of gravity, in N; mass is the amount of matter, in g and kg; Earth's gravity pulls on every kilogram with about 10 N, so 2 kg weighs 20 N; climbers and safety equipment; the kilogram standard 1889–2019.
- **Safety (home lab):** light kitchen things only, nothing heavy above toes, a grown-up handles glass jars, and keep magnets away from phones, bank cards and little children.
- ◇ **"Shape. Speed. Steer."**, **"Touching Gate, Distance Gate"**, **"Times ten for the newtons"** and **"Mass stays. Weight wanders."** are Volty-made tricks.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Ka Different forces (p165–167). Celly School's first lesson of 7K Forces.
- **Size:** 142 spoken lines (Volty 130, Bolt 12), 23 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1440 words. That is roughly 17–18 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice Autonoe).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ia.

**World:** The Arrow Arena, a giant sports stadium. When Volty says "Specs on", every object grows glowing red force arrows. The arrow points the way of the push or pull. A longer arrow means a bigger force. High above sits the referee, Newt, a small, very serious newt in a striped shirt, named after Isaac Newton. Newt holds the Net Force Scoreboard (used fully in 7Ke). Today is the Opening Ceremony: every force enters through the Touching Gate (contact) or the Distance Gate (non-contact).
- **Case:** "The case of the lighter astronaut" (the 7Je teaser).
- **Answered at the end:** the astronaut's mass, the amount of her, stays the same. Her weight is the pull of gravity. The Moon's gravity is weaker, so her weight is smaller.

**Main tricks:**
- "Shape. Speed. Steer." Squash your hands together, run on the spot, turn a steering wheel.
- "Touching Gate, Distance Gate." Two hands touching, then two hands apart.
- "Times ten for the newtons." Mass in kilograms, times ten, gives weight in newtons.
- "Mass stays. Weight wanders." Hold a fist still, then let the other hand float up.
