# 7Kd Pressure — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Bed of nails hook** (Kd00): ◇, a science-show stunt, not in the book. The script says clearly, twice, that only trained performers do it and that it must never be tried at home (Kd00_04, Kd12_17). On screen it's always a cartoon performer.
- ⚑ **Mars Climate Orbiter** (true story, Kd10): the book says only that the spacecraft crashed when it reached Mars, and broke up in its atmosphere, because two teams of engineers used different sets of units. The script adds ◇ that it was in 1999, and that one team used pounds of force while the other expected newtons (well documented). It avoids blaming any one person.
- ⚑ **Ötzi** (Kd07): the book says a body found in a glacier in 1991 was about 5300 years old, was named Ötzi, and that his shoes "may be" the oldest snowshoes. The script keeps "may". It doesn't mention how he died.
- ⚑ **Car tyres** (Kd07, book Q1): the photo C vehicle can't be seen in the scan. The script uses the standard idea that a vehicle on soft sand or snow needs wide tyres, to spread its weight (◇ check against photo C).
- **Book numbers:** Alex's crampons: 800 N on points with a total area of 0.2 cm², so 4000 N/cm². 1 Pa = 1 N/m². The road sign: 50 km/h in most of the world, 50 mph (about 80 km/h) in the UK. Champion snails: up to 3 mm/s. Kilo means 1000. 100 centimetres = 1 metre.
- **Book facts used:** pressure is the amount of force pushing on a certain area; high pressure makes you sink into snow, mud or sand; the four same-force/same-area rules; sharp knives; snowshoes; crampons; drawing pins; snowmobile tracks, skis and spikes; pressure = force ÷ area; N/m², pascal (Pa), N/cm²; Ötzi; SI units and why everyone uses them; the SI table; prefixes.
- **Safety (home lab):** light things only, a flour or sand tray, a play-dough press with a coin. Never press a sharp point into skin. Never try a bed of nails.
- ◇ **"Spread it, it's soft. Sharpen it, it's strong."**, **"Force over area"** and **"Same units, same planet"** are Volty-made tricks.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Kd Pressure (p172–173) and 7Kd Working Scientifically: SI units (p174–175). Celly School's fourth lesson of 7K Forces.
- **Size:** 138 spoken lines (Volty 127, Bolt 11), 22 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1410 words. That is roughly 16–17 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice Autonoe).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ia.

**World:** The Snowfield beside the Arrow Arena. Specs on: a person's big weight arrow splits into lots of tiny arrows, one for each square of area under them. A snowshoe has many squares, so each arrow is tiny and you don't sink. A crampon spike has one tiny square, so one giant arrow digs into the ice. Newt the referee watches from a snow-covered high chair.
- **Case:** "The case of the bed of nails" (the 7Kc teaser).
- **Answered at the end:** on a bed of nails, the performer's weight is spread over hundreds of points, so the pressure on each point is low. On one drawing pin, all the force pushes on one tiny point, so the pressure is very high.

**Main tricks:**
- "Spread it, it's soft. Sharpen it, it's strong." A flat palm pressed on the table; then one fingertip.
- "Force over area." Hand-chop: force sits on top, area underneath. Pressure = force ÷ area.
- "Same units, same planet." Everyone uses the same units, so everyone understands the numbers.

### Scene 5 · Force over area
