# 7Hd Making compounds — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Humphry Davy, 1807** (true story, Hd10) goes beyond the book ◇: he used electricity to get potassium, then sodium, out of their compounds. The famous detail that he "danced around the room" comes from his cousin's later account, so the script says "the story goes".
- ⚑ **Cereal iron home lab** (Hd11): only cereals fortified with iron powder work, and results vary by brand. Grown-ups should try it first. The strong magnet is handled by the grown-up only and kept away from small children (swallowing danger), phones and bank cards.
- ⚑ **Aluminium and iodine** (Hd09): this is a teacher demonstration in a fume cupboard (book photo). The script says clearly "never at home".
- **Iron sulfide** (book p127): it is a large regular structure of billions of atoms, not small molecules. The script says this simply ("a giant team", "where the map tears").
- ◇ **"Compounds Change Character"** and **"Mixture: Magnet can pick it. Compound: Can't"** are from the kit.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Hd Making compounds (p127–128). Celly School's fourth lesson of 7H.
- **Size:** 155 spoken lines (Atomy 140, Bolt 15), 35 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1550 words. That is roughly 19–20 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Atomy the atom (voice Zephyr).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ga.

**World:** Element City. When citizens from different houses hold hands tightly, they make a compound. That is a new team with a new character. Mixtures only mingle.
- **Where the map tears:** iron sulfide is not a small group holding hands. It is a giant, regular team of billions of atoms.
- **Case:** "The case of the missing magnetism" (the 7Hc teaser: "iron sticks to a magnet. Then its magnetism vanishes. Where does it go?").
- **Answered at the end:** the iron atoms are still there, but they joined with sulfur atoms. That made iron sulfide, a compound with new properties, and it is not magnetic.

**Main tricks:**
- "Compounds Change Character." (one hand changes into a different shape)
- "Mixture: Magnet can pick it. Compound: Can't." (pinch and lift / shake head)
- "Metal goes First, -IDE goes at the End." (point left / point right)

## Script

- **Hd00_03** [curious] Now the magnet does nothing. The magnetism has vanished!
- **Hd00_04** [warm] Today, we solve the case of the missing magnetism.

### Scene 2 · Compounds everywhere
- **Hd02_01** [clear] Remember, a compound has atoms of different elements joined together.
- **Hd02_02** [explaining] Most things around you are compounds. Very few things are pure elements.
- **Hd02_03** [explaining] The most common compound in the Earth's crust is silicon dioxide.
- **Hd02_04** [explaining] Silicon dioxide is in sand, and in many rocks.
- **Hd02_05** [explaining] Carbon is a champion joiner. It forms over ten million compounds!
- **Hd02_06 BOLT** [BOLT: amazed, robotic] Ten million? Carbon must have a very big address book.
- **Hd02_07** [warm, amused] It does, Bolt. Many of those compounds are in living things, like you and me.
- **Hd02_08 BOLT** [BOLT: proud, robotic] Not me. I am mostly metal. And charm.
- **Hd02_09** [curious] So how do we make a new compound? Let's watch two elements meet.
- **Hd02_10** [gentle] Hold hands with yourself. Two different hands, joined. A compound!
*(long pause)*

### Scene 3 · Iron meets sulfur
- **Hd03_01** [clear] Here is grey iron powder. And here is yellow sulfur powder.
- **Hd03_02** [explaining] We stir them together. This is a mixture. The atoms are not joined.
- **Hd03_03** [curious] Can we separate this mixture? Bolt, bring your magnet!
*(long pause)*
- **Hd03_04 BOLT** [BOLT: excited, robotic] Whoosh! The iron jumped onto my magnet. The yellow sulfur stayed behind.
- **Hd03_05** [pleased] Yes! In a mixture, the iron is still iron. It is still magnetic.
- **Hd03_06** [explaining] Now we heat the mixture. Heating starts the change.
- **Hd03_07** [explaining] The mixture begins to glow. The glow spreads, even after we stop heating.
- **Hd03_08** [explaining] It gives out heat of its own. A new substance is forming.
- **Hd03_09** [explaining] This is a chemical reaction. A change that makes new substances.
- **Hd03_10** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Chem. I. Cal. Re. Ac. Tion. Six taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Hd03_11** [explaining] In the reaction, iron atoms and sulfur atoms join with bonds.
- **Hd03_12** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Bond. One tap.
*(long pause)*
- **Hd03_13** [explaining] A bond is a force that holds atoms together. Like holding hands, very tightly.
- **Hd03_14** [gentle] Clasp your hands together. Pull. They stay joined. A bond!
*(long pause)*
- **Hd03_15 BOLT** [BOLT: curious, robotic] So the heat is like pressing the start button?
- **Hd03_16** [pleased] Exactly, Bolt. Heat starts it. Then the reaction keeps itself going.

### Scene 4 · A new character
- **Hd04_01** [clear] The new substance is called iron sulfide.
- **Hd04_02** [explaining] It is black. It is not grey like iron. It is not yellow like sulfur.
- **Hd04_03 BOLT** [BOLT: very sure, robotic] It still has iron in it. So my magnet will pick it up!
- **Hd04_04** [curious] Let's see, Bolt. Try it.
*(long pause)*
- **Hd04_05 BOLT** [BOLT: surprised, robotic] Nothing! It ignores my magnet. Rebooting!
- **Hd04_06** [explaining] Iron sulfide is not magnetic. A compound has new properties.
- **Hd04_07** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Prop. Er. Ties. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Hd04_08** [explaining] Properties are what a substance is like, and how it behaves.
- **Hd04_09** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Compounds Change Character.
- **Hd04_10** [gentle] Make a fist. Now change it into a flat hand. A new character!
*(long pause)*
- **Hd04_11** [clear] Here is where our city map tears a little.
- **Hd04_12** [explaining] Iron sulfide is not made of small groups holding hands.
- **Hd04_13** [explaining] It is one giant, regular team of billions of atoms, all joined.
- **Hd04_14** [explaining] Like a whole city square of citizens, all holding hands in neat rows.

### Scene 5 · Mixture or compound?
- **Hd05_01** [clear] So how can you tell a mixture from a compound?
- **Hd05_02** [explaining] In a mixture, each element keeps its own properties. The atoms only mingle.
- **Hd05_03** [explaining] A mixture can be separated easily. A magnet pulls out the iron.
- **Hd05_04** [explaining] In a compound, the atoms are bonded. It has new properties.
- **Hd05_05** [explaining] A compound cannot be separated by a magnet, or by filtering.
- **Hd05_06** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Mixture: Magnet can pick it. Compound: Can't.
- **Hd05_07** [gentle] Pinch the air and lift, for mixture. Shake your head, for compound.
*(long pause)*
- **Hd05_08** [curious] Salt water. Can you get the salt back by evaporating? Mixture or compound?
*(long pause)*
- **Hd05_09** [pleased] A mixture! The salt and water only mingle.
- **Hd05_10 BOLT** [BOLT: thinking, robotic] Mingle means mixture. Hold hands tightly means compound. Saving to memory!
- **Hd05_11** [curious] Sand in water. Can you filter the sand out? Mixture or compound?
*(long pause)*
- **Hd05_12** [pleased] A mixture! But the sand itself is silicon dioxide. That is a compound.
