# 7Hb Earth's elements — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Mendeleev and gallium** (hook and true story, Hb00, Hb09): accurate but not in the book. In 1869 Mendeleev arranged the elements known then into a table and left gaps, predicting what the missing elements would be like. In 1875 the French chemist Lecoq de Boisbaudran found gallium, and it matched Mendeleev's predictions closely. The popular story that he laid out element cards like a card game (or dreamt the table) is not well documented, so the script says "he is often pictured…".
- ◇ **Gallium melts at about 30 °C**, below body temperature (about 37 °C). Standard value, not in the book. The book names gallium only as a smart-phone element in short supply.
- ⚑ **Symbol origins:** Fe, Na, Au, Cu, Ag and Pb come from old Latin names (*ferrum, natrium, aurum, cuprum, argentum, plumbum*). The book only asks for elements whose symbol and first letter differ. The script says "from old Latin names" and gives only *aurum* and *ferrum*.
- ◇ **"Gold makes you say Au!"** and **"Oxygen and Silicon Own the crust"** are Atomy-made jokes and tricks, not facts.
- **Earth's crust** (book diagram A): oxygen and silicon make up about 75%, then aluminium, iron and calcium. The script gives only "about three quarters".
- **Dalton** (book): John Dalton (1766–1844) used circles, marks and letters; other scientists found them awkward. The Activity Pack answers give the circle with a cross as sulfur, the circle with an upside-down Y as phosphorus and the circle with a dot as hydrogen (SB Q5).
- **Oxygen flame** (book photo C): oxygen helps a gas burn at 3500 °C, hot enough to cut metal.
- **Smart phones** (book): indium, gallium and antimony are in short supply; about £150 million of valuable metals in discarded UK phones.
- **Home lab:** a grown-up handles any wire (old, unplugged cable only). No batteries opened.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Hb Earth's elements (p122–123). Celly School's second lesson of 7H.
- **Size:** 156 spoken lines (Atomy 141, Bolt 15), 36 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1620 words. That is roughly 20–21 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Atomy the atom (voice Zephyr).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ga.

**World:** Element City, the map room. The periodic table is the city map. Every house has a name sign with its symbol. Atomy finds one house with no sign on the door yet: Mendeleev's gap.
- **Case:** "The case of the empty house" (the 7Ha teaser: "a metal that melts in your hand, and a scientist who predicted it").
- **Answered at the end:** Mendeleev arranged the elements in a table and left gaps where elements seemed to be missing. He predicted what the missing element would be like. Gallium was found in 1875 and matched his predictions. It melts at about 30 °C, so it would melt in your hand.

**Main tricks:**
- "Big letter Boss, little letter follows." (tall hand, then short hand) and "Two capitals, two elements." (Co is cobalt; CO is carbon and oxygen)
- "Oxygen and Silicon Own the crust." (two hands make a big ball)
- "Same atoms, different joins." (diamond and graphite: fingers locked tight, then hands sliding apart)
