# 7Ha The air we breathe — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Rayleigh and Ramsay** (hook and true story, Ha00, Ha09): the argon discovery is accurate but not in the book. Rayleigh found that nitrogen taken from air was always slightly heavier (about half a percent) than nitrogen made from chemicals. With William Ramsay he found argon in 1894. Both won Nobel Prizes in 1904. The script says "a tiny bit heavier" and keeps the dates.
- ⚑ **Argon means "lazy"**: from the Greek *argos*, idle. Accurate, and kept as one line.
- ⚑ **Air percentages:** the book's text says 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, 0.9% argon and 0.1% other gases. Its table says oxygen 20.95%, argon 0.93% and carbon dioxide 0.039% (today about 0.04%). The script follows the text (78, 21, just under 1) and says carbon dioxide is "a tiny amount, much less than one percent".
- ⚑ **Elements:** the book says about 90 natural and 28 manufactured elements, and that the newest is number 117, Tennessine. Oganesson (118) was named at the same time (2016), so the script says "one of the newest".
- ◇ **Bead model of air** and the **kitchen change sort** (home lab) go beyond the book. The cut apple going brown is a chemical change ◇.
- ◇ **"Physical? Put it back. Chemical? Can't come back."**, **"Qualitative is Quality words. Quantitative is Quantity numbers."** and the unit chant **"Atom Alone. Molecule Mates. Compound Combines. Mixture's just Mingling."** are Atomy-made tricks.
- **Candle investigation** (book): beaker volumes 100, 200, 400, 600 cm³ and burning times 5.1, 7.5, 13.0, 17.9 s. The script describes the pattern only ("the bigger the beaker, the longer it burns") and the line of best fit.
- **Metals running out** (book table D): nickel 90 years, copper 61, zinc 46. Kept in Scene 4.
- **Home lab:** small beads are kept away from younger children (choking). A grown-up uses the toaster.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Ha Our material world (p118), 7Ha Sorting resource data (p119) and 7Ha The air we breathe (p120–121). Celly School's first lesson of 7H Atoms, elements and compounds.
- **Size:** 158 spoken lines (Atomy 145, Bolt 13), 32 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1760 words. That is roughly 21–22 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Atomy the atom (voice Zephyr).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ga.

**World:** Element City opens. The periodic table is a city map. Every element has its own house, with its symbol on the door. Everyone in one house is identical. Atoms are the citizens. Molecules are citizens holding hands in a group of a set size. Compounds are citizens from different houses holding hands. Mixtures are crowds walking down the same street without holding hands.
- **Case:** "The case of the heavy nitrogen" (the 7Ge teaser: "A tiny weighing puzzle uncovered a hidden gas. You breathe it every day!").
- **Answered at the end:** argon. Air is a mixture. The "nitrogen" taken from air had argon atoms hiding in it, so it weighed a tiny bit more.

**Main tricks:**
- Unit chant: "Atom Alone. Molecule Mates. Compound Combines. Mixture's just Mingling." (one finger up / two fingers linked / two hands clasped / hands wander past each other)
- "Physical? Put it back. Chemical? Can't come back." (hand rewinds / flat palm stops like a wall)
- "Qualitative is Quality words. Quantitative is Quantity numbers." (speech-bubble hand / count on fingers)

### Scene 2 · Physical changes and chemical reactions (Bolt: you can unfry an egg)
- **Ha02_01** [clear] We use materials from the Earth every day. Sometimes we change them.
- **Ha02_02** [explaining] Some changes make no new substance. These are called physical changes.
- **Ha02_03** [explaining] Melting metal, freezing water, mixing sand and water. No new substance is made.
- **Ha02_04** [explaining] Physical changes can often be reversed quite easily. Melt it, then let it set again.
- **Ha02_05** [explaining] Remember evaporation? We can get salt back from sea water. Another physical change.
- **Ha02_06** [explaining] Other changes always make one or more new substances. These are chemical reactions.
- **Ha02_07** [explaining] Mix an alkali with fat, and a reaction makes a new substance. Soap!
- **Ha02_08** [explaining] Chemical reactions are often hard to reverse. Like frying an egg, or iron rusting.
- **Ha02_09 BOLT** [BOLT: very sure, robotic] I fried an egg for breakfast. I will just unfry it. Easy!
- **Ha02_10** [curious] Hmm. Can a fried egg ever go back to a runny egg?
