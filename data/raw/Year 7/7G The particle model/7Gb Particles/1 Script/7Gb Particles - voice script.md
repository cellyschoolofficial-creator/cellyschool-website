# 7Gb Particles — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Empty space between gas particles** ("Where the map tears", Gb05_08–10): the book says gas particles are "far apart". That the space between them is empty (not air) is standard KS3 ◇ and is the classic misconception fix. Keep or cut.
- ⚑ **"Heated particles don't get bigger"** (Gb08_09–11): the book only asks what heating does to the particles (answer: "gives them more movement"). The "they don't grow" line is a standard KS3 misconception fix ◇.
- ⚑ **Democritus:** the Activity Pack (7Gb-9) names Parmenides and Democritus. The script says "about two and a half thousand years ago" (Democritus lived about 460–370 BC) and "his idea was only a hypothesis". It took more than 2,000 years of evidence to become a theory.
- ◇ **"Tiny. Travelling. Tugging. Tug-strength changes."** is an Atomy-made version of the kit's "Particles Move, Attract, Vary".
- ◇ **Club Particle dancers** are an analogy. The script says plainly that real particles do not choose, never stop and never get tired (Gb05_11).
- **Ice boxes cartoon** (SB Q2): told as a hypothesis to test. Atomy says it partly explains melting but cannot say what holds the boxes together, or where the walls go.
- **Home lab:** warm tap water only, poured by a grown-up. No boiling water. No tasting.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Gb Hypotheses and theories (Working Scientifically, p107) and 7Gb Particles (p108–109). Celly School's second lesson of unit 7G.
- **Size:** 160 spoken lines (Atomy 143, Bolt 17), 33 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1710 words. That is roughly 20 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Atomy the atom (voice Zephyr).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ga.

**World:** Club Particle. Tonight the Zoom Lens goes inside and we meet the dancers: the particles. Floor S (Statue Shuffle) dancers hold hands tightly and wobble on the spot. Floor L (Conga Slide) dancers stay close and slide past each other. Floor G (Bumper Zoom) dancers are far apart and zoom in all directions. DJ Heat turns the music up: everyone moves more.
- **Case:** "The case of the solid wall" (the 7Ga teaser: "Why can you walk through air, but not a wall?").
- **Answered at the end:** air, water and walls are all made of particles. In a wall (a solid) strong forces hold the particles close in fixed places, so you cannot push between them. In air (a gas) the particles are far apart with weak forces, so you move through easily.

**Main tricks:**
- Unit chant: "Solids Shake. Liquids Slide. Gases Go everywhere."
- "Hypothesis Hangs on 'depends on'. Prediction Points: if… then…" (hand hangs from a hook; then point forward)
- Particle theory in four: "Tiny. Travelling. Tugging. Tug-strength changes." (pinch fingers; wiggle; link fingers and pull; loosen the grip)
- Recall from 7Ga: "Shape, Space, Spill, Squash."
