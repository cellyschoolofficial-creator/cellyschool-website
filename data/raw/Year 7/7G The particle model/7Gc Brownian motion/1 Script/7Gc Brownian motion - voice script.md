# 7Gc Brownian motion — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **What Brown saw:** historians say Brown watched tiny particles released from pollen grains, not whole grains. The book says "pollen grains", so the script follows the book.
- ⚑ **Brown's tests:** the Activity Pack (7Gc-2) says Brown first thought the pollen was alive, then used 100-year-old pollen, which moved the same way. The script uses those two facts, plus "dust and soot" (Activity Pack 7Gc-3 cartoon: soot).
- ⚑ **Dates beyond the book:** Einstein's explanation in 1905 and Perrin's experiments around 1908 are standard ◇. The book gives only their life dates. The script says "nearly eighty years later" for Einstein and gives no year for Perrin. Perrin's 1926 Nobel Prize is left out.
- ◇ **Sunbeam dust** (Gc06_08–09, true-or-false): the sparkly dust you see in a sunbeam is mostly moved by air currents. Real Brownian specks are far smaller and need a microscope. This is a standard correction, not in the book.
- ◇ **"Bumped, not Brave"** and **"Random hits, Random path"** are Atomy-made tricks.
- ⚑ **Big number:** the book Fact "about 33 500 000 000 000 000 000 000 000 water particles in a 1-litre bottle" is said as "more than thirty million billion billion" (Gc08_10). Check you're happy with that wording.
- **Nanoscale sizes** are the book's (diagram E): water particle 0.3 nm, sugar 1 nm, virus 65 nm, pollen grain 20 000 nm, a dot on paper 500 000 nm, a football 22 cm.
- **Home lab:** a ping-pong ball on dried rice in a baking tray is a model only. The microscope bonus is not promised. Nothing is eaten.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Gc Brownian motion (p110–111). Celly School's third lesson of unit 7G.
- **Size:** 159 spoken lines (Atomy 142, Bolt 17), 33 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1700 words. That is roughly 20 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Atomy the atom (voice Zephyr).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ga.

**World:** Club Particle. A giant beach ball (the speck) lands on Floor G, the Bumper Zoom, and is shoved about by dancers too small to see.
- **Case:** "The case of the dancing speck" (the 7Gb teaser: "Tiny specks in water jiggle all by themselves. Are they alive?").
- **Answered at the end:** no. The specks are hit, all the time, by invisible moving particles of water or air. More hits on one side push the speck that way, and the hits are random, so the speck jerks about. This was strong evidence for the particle theory.

**Main tricks:**
- "Brownian means Bumped, not Brave." The speck does not move itself; it is bumped. (sway as if nudged by an invisible crowd)
- "Random hits, Random path." (zig-zag a finger in the air)
- "Nano knows nine zeros." A nanometre is a billionth of a metre. (flash nine fingers, then tap)
- Recall from 7Gb: "Tiny. Travelling. Tugging. Tug-strength changes." "Hypothesis Hangs on depends on."
