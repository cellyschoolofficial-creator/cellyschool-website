# 7Ga Solids, liquids and gases — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Archimedes and the golden crown:** this is a legend, written down about 200 years later by the Roman writer Vitruvius. Historians doubt the bath and "Eureka!" details. The script says "the famous story says" and "it may be a legend" (Ga09_01, Ga09_07). Archimedes himself was real.
- ◇ **Density tower (home lab):** honey, water and oil are standard kitchen layers. The script says "heavier for its size" and gives no numbers for honey or oil (honey ≈ 1.4 g/cm³, oil ≈ 0.9 g/cm³ are not in the book).
- ◇ **"Shape, Space, Spill, Squash"** (the four property questions) and **"Dense means Crowded"** are Atomy-made tricks, not from the book.
- ⚑ **Club Particle** (three dance floors and DJ Heat) is introduced as a place only. The dancers (particles) are explained in 7Gb. In this lesson, Atomy promises "next time we zoom in and meet the dancers", so the particle theory is not taught before its lesson.
- ⚑ **Sand:** the book says sand "flows like a liquid as it is made up of small pieces". The script follows that and says each grain keeps its own shape (Ga03_10–12).
- **Biodiesel Fact** (EU diesel contains 5–10% biodiesel) is kept as one line (Ga04_08).
- **Waste numbers** (5 tonnes per person per year in Europe; over half to landfill) are the book's (p104). Said as "about five tonnes".
- **Home lab:** needle-free medicine syringe only. A grown-up pours the honey and oil. Nothing is tasted.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Ga Sorting rubbish (p104, unit opener) and 7Ga Solids, liquids and gases (p105–106). Celly School's first lesson of unit 7G.
- **Size:** 160 spoken lines (Atomy 143, Bolt 17), 36 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1640 words. That is roughly 20 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Atomy the atom (voice Zephyr).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar** (same as 7F): Atomy asks a prediction, then Bolt gives a confident wrong idea, then the demo tests it on screen. Next the Zoom Lens shows why, then a memory trick or gesture, then quick retrieval. Bolt is never mocked. He reboots happily and gets the true-or-false right by the end.

**World:** Club Particle, a three-floor dance club next to a landfill site. Floor S is the Statue Shuffle (solids), Floor L the Conga Slide (liquids), Floor G the Bumper Zoom (gases). DJ Heat runs the music. This lesson tours the floors from the outside; the dancers are met in 7Gb.
- **Case:** "The case of the pouring sand" (the 7Fe teaser: "You can pour sand like water. So is sand a liquid?").
- **Answered at the end:** sand is a solid. It is made of lots of small pieces. Each grain keeps its own shape and volume; it is the pieces that flow.

**Main tricks:**
- Unit chant: "Solids Shake. Liquids Slide. Gases Go everywhere." (shiver on the spot; hands glide past each other; arms fling wide)
- "Shape, Space, Spill, Squash." The four property questions: keeps its shape? keeps its space (volume)? spills (flows)? squashes (compresses)? (tick four fingers)
- "Dense means Crowded." (squeeze your shoulders in)
- Recall from 7Fe: "Never Mix, Never Risk." "Every Alkali is a Base."
