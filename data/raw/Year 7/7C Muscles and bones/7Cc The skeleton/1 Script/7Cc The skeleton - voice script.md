# 7Cc The skeleton — voice script

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book p44–45. 117 lines, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts. Voice: Sulafat (Gemini Flash TTS). No sentence is longer than 15 words.

## Pearson coverage
| Pearson point | Where |
|---|---|
| Bones are living organs; grow and repair; fracture (SB, AP 7Cc-1) | Scene 2 |
| Bone cells need nutrients and oxygen for respiration (SB Q1) | Scene 2 |
| Inside a bone: compact bone tube (hollow, strong, light), spongy bone, marrow, cartilage (SB Q2, Q3; AP 7Cc-9 Q2) | Scene 3 |
| Old people's bones get lighter / more fragile (AP 7Cc-9 Q2c) | Scene 3 |
| Functions: support, protection, movement + making blood cells (SB Q9, AP 7Cc-1 Q3) | Scenes 4–5 |
| Backbone = vertebrae, main support; ribs support lungs (SB Q4–6) | Scene 4 |
| Skull protects brain (fixed joints, 22 bones); backbone protects spinal cord; ribcage protects heart and lungs; knee cap protects joint (SB Q5–7, AP 7Cc-8 Q2) | Scene 5 |
| Joints; tendons (muscle to bone); ligaments (bone to bone); cartilage slippery (WB Q5, AP 7Cc-9 Q3) | Scene 7 |
| Fixed, hinge, ball and socket joints with examples (SB Q8, WB Q6, AP 7Cc-8 Q5) | Scenes 8, 11 |
| Baby ~270 bones → adult 206; skull gaps let brain grow; bones fuse into fixed joints (AP 7Cc-9 Q1e, 7Cc-10 Q4) | Scenes 0, 9 |
| Injuries: fracture, sprain (ligament), dislocation, pulled muscle (SB Q10) | Scene 10 |

Workbook-only: endoskeleton / exoskeleton / hydrostatic skeleton comparison (AP 7Cc-10), labelling a full skeleton, vertebrae gaps in a baby's backbone.

## Script

### Scene 0
- **Cc00_01** [curious, intriguing] Here is a mystery. A new baby has about two hundred and seventy bones.
- **Cc00_02** [curious] But a grown-up has only two hundred and six. Where did the other bones go?
- **Cc00_03** [warm] Today, we solve the case of the vanishing bones.

### Scene 1
- **Cc01_01** [warm, smiling] Hello, scientist! Welcome back, detective.
- **Cc01_02** [calm, recalling] Last time, we found out how the heart pumps blood.
- **Cc01_03** [clear] Which way do arteries carry blood?
*(long pause)*
- **Cc01_04** [pleased] Away from the heart. Arteries go Away.
- **Cc01_05** [clear] And where are blood cells made?
*(long pause)*
- **Cc01_06** [pleased] In the bone marrow, inside your bones.
- **Cc01_07** [curious] So bones make living cells. Does that mean bones are alive?
- **Cc01_08** [gentle] Press gently on your arm. Can you feel the hard bone under your skin?
*(long pause)*

### Scene 2
- **Cc02_01** [clear] Yes! Bones are alive. Each bone is an organ.
- **Cc02_02** [explaining] Bones grow as you grow. If a bone breaks, it can repair itself.
- **Cc02_03** [explaining] A broken bone is called a fracture.
- **Cc02_04** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Frac. Ture. Two taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cc02_05** [explaining] Bones contain bone-making cells. These cells need oxygen and food.
- **Cc02_06** [explaining] Why? They use them for respiration, to release energy.
- **Cc02_07** [clear] Bones are hard and strong. But they are also quite light.
- **Cc02_08** [curious] How can something be strong and light at the same time? Let's look inside.
- **Cc02_09** [gentle] Tap your knuckles gently on the table. Bones are hard and strong.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 3
- **Cc03_01** [explaining] Here is a bone cut open. It has different tissues, like every organ.
- **Cc03_02** [clear] On the outside is compact bone. It is very hard and strong, but heavy.
- **Cc03_03** [explaining] Compact bone is shaped like a tube, with a hollow middle.
- **Cc03_04** [explaining] A tube is strong, but much lighter than a solid rod.
- **Cc03_05** [clear] Inside is spongy bone. It has lots of tiny spaces, which keep the bone light.
- **Cc03_06** [clear] In the middle is the bone marrow. It makes new blood cells.
- **Cc03_07** [clear] And on the ends is cartilage. It is smooth and slippery.
- **Cc03_08** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Car. Ti. Lage. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cc03_09** [explaining] As people get very old, their bones can get lighter and more fragile.
- **Cc03_10** [gentle] Roll a piece of paper into a tube. Feel how stiff it is.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 4
- **Cc04_01** [clear] All your bones together make your skeleton.
- **Cc04_02** [explaining] The skeleton has three main jobs. Here is a trick to remember them.
- **Cc04_03** [slow, rhythmic] Some. People. Move.
- **Cc04_04** [explaining] S for support. P for protection. M for movement.
- **Cc04_05** [clear] First, support. Your skeleton holds your body up and gives it shape.
- **Cc04_06** [explaining] Without a skeleton, you would flop like a jellyfish!
- **Cc04_07** [explaining] Your main support is your backbone. It is made of small bones called vertebrae.
- **Cc04_08** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Ver. Te. Brae. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cc04_09** [explaining] We say ver-tuh-bray. One small bone is called a vertebra.
- **Cc04_10** [explaining] Your ribs support your lungs. Without them, the lungs would collapse.
- **Cc04_11** [encouraging] Say the trick with me. Some. People. Move.
- **Cc04_12** [gentle] Sit up tall. Feel your backbone holding you up.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 5
- **Cc05_01** [clear] The second job is protection. Bones protect soft organs inside you.
- **Cc05_02** [explaining] Your skull protects your brain.
- **Cc05_03** [explaining] The skull is twenty-two bones, locked tightly together. They cannot move.
- **Cc05_04** [explaining] Your backbone protects your spinal cord. That is a bundle of nerves.
- **Cc05_05** [explaining] Your ribcage protects your heart and lungs.
- **Cc05_06** [explaining] And your knee cap protects your knee joint.
- **Cc05_07** [clear] Remember, your bones have one more job. The bone marrow makes blood cells.
- **Cc05_08** [gentle] Put your hands on your head. Then on your ribs. Both are bone helmets!
*(long pause)*

### Scene 6
- **Cc06_01** [playful] Thumbs up if it is true. Thumbs down if it is false.
- **Cc06_02** [clear] Number one. Bones are not alive.
*(long pause)*
- **Cc06_03** [pleased] Thumbs down! Bones are living organs. They grow and repair.
- **Cc06_04** [clear] Number two. The skull protects the brain.
*(long pause)*
- **Cc06_05** [pleased] Thumbs up!
- **Cc06_06** [clear] Number three. Spongy bone makes bones heavier.
*(long pause)*
- **Cc06_07** [pleased] Thumbs down! Its tiny spaces keep bones light.
- **Cc06_08** [warm, proud] Brilliant detective work. Part one is done. Time for a break!

### Scene 7
- **Cc07_01** [warm] Welcome back, detective. Now for the third job. Movement.
- **Cc07_02** [explaining] Where two bones meet, there is a joint. Many joints can bend.
- **Cc07_03** [explaining] Muscles move your bones. A muscle is joined to a bone by a tendon.
- **Cc07_04** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Ten. Don. Two taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cc07_05** [explaining] The bones in a joint are held together by ligaments.
- **Cc07_06** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Lig. A. Ment. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cc07_07** [clear] Here is a trick. Tendons join muscle to bone. Ligaments link bone to bone.
- **Cc07_08** [explaining] Cartilage covers the ends of the bones. It is slippery, so the bones slide easily.
- **Cc07_09** [gentle] Bend your knee slowly. Muscles, tendons, ligaments and cartilage are all working.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 8
- **Cc08_01** [clear] There are different kinds of joint.
- **Cc08_02** [clear] A fixed joint does not move. The bones of your skull have fixed joints.
- **Cc08_03** [clear] A hinge joint moves back and forth in one direction, like a door.
- **Cc08_04** [explaining] Your elbow and your knee are hinge joints.
- **Cc08_05** [clear] A ball and socket joint can move in almost any direction.
- **Cc08_06** [explaining] The round end of one bone sits in a cup in the other bone.
- **Cc08_07** [explaining] Your hip and your shoulder are ball and socket joints.
- **Cc08_08** [gentle] Bend your elbow like a door. Now swing your arm round in a big circle.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 9
- **Cc09_01** [curious] Now, back to our mystery. Where do a baby's bones go?
- **Cc09_02** [explaining] A baby's skull has gaps between the bones. This lets the brain keep growing.
- **Cc09_03** [explaining] As a child grows, some small bones join together. They fuse into one bone.
- **Cc09_04** [explaining] The skull bones fuse, and make fixed joints.
- **Cc09_05** [explaining] Some bones at the bottom of the backbone fuse too.
- **Cc09_06** [explaining] So no bones vanish. Small bones join to make bigger bones.
- **Cc09_07** [warm] Case nearly solved! A baby has about two hundred and seventy. A grown-up has two hundred and six.
- **Cc09_08** [gentle] Press your two hands together, palm to palm. Two bones fuse into one.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 10
- **Cc10_01** [explaining] Sometimes, bones and joints get hurt.
- **Cc10_02** [clear] A fracture is a broken bone. A cast holds the bone still while it repairs.
- **Cc10_03** [clear] A sprain is when a ligament is stretched or torn.
- **Cc10_04** [clear] A dislocation is when bones are pushed out of line in a joint.
- **Cc10_05** [explaining] A pulled muscle is a small tear in a muscle or a tendon.
- **Cc10_06** [explaining] Athletes warm up first, to help keep their joints and muscles safe.
- **Cc10_07** [gentle] Roll your wrists and ankles slowly, like an athlete warming up.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 11
- **Cc11_01** [playful] Detective game! Which kind of joint is it? Fixed, hinge, or ball and socket?
- **Cc11_02** [clear] Number one. Your elbow.
*(long pause)*
- **Cc11_03** [pleased] Hinge! It moves in one direction.
- **Cc11_04** [clear] Number two. Your hip.
*(long pause)*
- **Cc11_05** [pleased] Ball and socket! It moves in almost any direction.
- **Cc11_06** [clear] Number three. The bones of your skull.
*(long pause)*
- **Cc11_07** [pleased] Fixed! They do not move.
- **Cc11_08** [clear] Bonus question. Which job does the ribcage do?
*(long pause)*
- **Cc11_09** [pleased] Protection! It protects the heart and lungs. It also supports the lungs.

### Scene 12
- **Cc12_01** [upbeat, friendly] Quiz time! Say the answer out loud.
- **Cc12_02** [clear] Question one. What are the three main jobs of the skeleton?
*(long pause)*
- **Cc12_03** [pleased] Support, protection and movement. Some People Move.
- **Cc12_04** [clear] Question two. What does bone marrow make?
*(long pause)*
- **Cc12_05** [pleased] Blood cells.
- **Cc12_06** [clear] Question three. What joins a muscle to a bone?
*(long pause)*
- **Cc12_07** [pleased] A tendon.
- **Cc12_08** [clear] Question four. What holds the bones in a joint together?
*(long pause)*
- **Cc12_09** [pleased] Ligaments.
- **Cc12_10** [clear] Question five. What kind of joint is your shoulder?
*(long pause)*
- **Cc12_11** [pleased] A ball and socket joint.
- **Cc12_12** [warm, calm] Here are three big ideas.
- **Cc12_13** [warm, calm] One. Bones are living organs. They are strong, but light.
- **Cc12_14** [warm, calm] Two. The skeleton has three jobs. Support, protection and movement.
- **Cc12_15** [warm, calm] Three. Joints can be fixed, hinge, or ball and socket.
- **Cc12_16** [warm] Case solved, detective! No bones vanish. Small bones fuse together.
- **Cc12_17** [warm] Next time, we find out how muscles move your bones.
- **Cc12_18** [warm, cheerful] Great work today. See you next time, scientist!
