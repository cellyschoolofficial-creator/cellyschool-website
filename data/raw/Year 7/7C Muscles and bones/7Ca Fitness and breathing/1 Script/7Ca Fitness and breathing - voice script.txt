## 0
[curious, intriguing] Here is a mystery. You breathe about twenty thousand times every day.
[curious] But your lungs cannot move on their own.
[warm] So how do you breathe? Today, we solve the case of the moving chest.
## 1
[warm, smiling] Hello, scientist! Welcome back, detective.
[bright] Today, we start a brand new unit. Muscles and bones.
[explaining] In this unit, we visit sports scientists. They study how athletes' bodies work.
[calm, recalling] But first, let's remember the building blocks of the body.
[clear] What is the smallest building block?
[long pause]
[pleased] A cell!
[clear] What do we call a group of cells doing the same job?
[long pause]
[pleased] A tissue.
[calm, recalling] Different tissues work together to make an organ.
[calm, recalling] And organs work together in an organ system.
[slow, rhythmic] Cell. Tissue. Organ. Organ system.
[gentle] Build it with your hands. One finger, then a fist, then two fists together.
[long pause]
## 2
[explaining] Athletes need to be fit. But what does fit mean?
[clear] Being fit means your body can do the things your life needs.
[explaining] Sports scientists talk about four S-factors.
[slow, rhythmic] Suppleness. Strength. Speed. Stamina.
[explaining] Suppleness means bending and stretching easily. A dancer needs suppleness.
[explaining] Strength means using a big force. A judo player needs strength.
[explaining] Speed means moving fast. A wheelchair racer needs speed.
[clear] Stamina means keeping going for a long time without getting tired.
[slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Stam. I. Na. Three taps.
[long pause]
[explaining] A bike rider on a long race needs stamina.
[encouraging] Say the four S-factors with me. Suppleness. Strength. Speed. Stamina.
[gentle] Hold up four fingers. One for each S.
[long pause]

## 4
[curious] Now, why do we breathe at all?
[explaining] Every cell in your body needs energy.
[explaining] Cells release energy from food. This is called respiration.
[slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Res. Pi. Ra. Tion. Four taps.
[long pause]
[explaining] Respiration needs oxygen. Oxygen is a gas in the air.
[explaining] About one fifth of the air is oxygen.
[explaining] Respiration also makes a waste gas. It is called carbon dioxide.
[explaining] We get rid of carbon dioxide when we breathe out.
[clear] Here is a common mistake. Breathing is not respiration.
[explaining] Breathing moves air in and out. Respiration releases energy inside cells.
[gentle] Hold your hand in front of your mouth. Breathe out. Feel the warm air leave your body.
[long pause]
## 5
[explaining] Oxygen and carbon dioxide swap places in the lungs.
[clear] This swap is called gas exchange.
[explaining] Oxygen goes from the air into the blood. Carbon dioxide leaves the blood.
[explaining] One gas is exchanged for the other.
[clear] The organs that do this make the gas exchange system. Let's meet them.
[explaining] Air goes in and out through a tube called the trachea.
[slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Tra. Che. A. Three taps.
[long pause]
[explaining] We say tray-key-uh. Its everyday name is the windpipe.
[explaining] The trachea leads to two lungs. Gas exchange happens in the lungs.
[explaining] The lungs sit inside your ribs. The ribs keep them safe.
[gentle] Touch the front of your neck gently. Your windpipe is just under there.
[long pause]

[clear] Number one. Breathing and respiration are the same thing.
[long pause]
[pleased] Thumbs down! Breathing moves air. Respiration releases energy in cells.
[clear] Number two. About one fifth of the air is oxygen.
[long pause]
[pleased] Thumbs up!
[clear] Number three. In the lungs, oxygen goes into the blood.
[long pause]

## 7
[warm] Welcome back, detective. Now, back to our mystery.
[curious] Your lungs cannot move themselves. So what moves them? Muscles!
[explaining] Muscle tissue is made of muscle cells. They can change shape.
[explaining] Each muscle cell is packed with long strands.
[clear] When the strands pull together, the cell gets shorter and fatter. It contracts.
[slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Con. Tract. Two taps.
[long pause]
[explaining] Con means together. Tract means pull.
[explaining] When the cell goes back to its first shape, it relaxes.
[explaining] All the cells in a muscle contract and relax together.
[clear] A muscle is an organ. It has muscle tissue, nerve tissue and wrapping tissue.
[gentle] Squeeze your fist tight. That is contract. Now let it go. That is relax.
[long pause]
## 8
[explaining] Two kinds of muscle help you breathe.
[clear] The first kind sit between your ribs. They are the intercostal muscles.
[slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] In. Ter. Cos. Tal. Four taps.
[long pause]
[explaining] Inter means between. Costa means rib. So intercostal means between the ribs.
[clear] The second is a sheet of muscle under your lungs. It is the diaphragm.
[slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Di. A. Phragm. Three taps.
[long pause]
[explaining] We say dye-uh-fram. The g at the end is silent.
[explaining] The diaphragm is shaped like a dome. It sits between your chest and your stomach.
[gentle] Put your hands on your ribs. The intercostal muscles are under your fingers.
[long pause]
## 9
[clear] Now let's breathe in. Breathing in is called inhalation.
[slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] In. Ha. La. Tion. Four taps.
[long pause]
[explaining] First, the intercostal muscles contract. They pull the ribs up and out.
[explaining] At the same time, the diaphragm muscles contract. The diaphragm moves down and flattens.
[explaining] Now there is more space inside your chest. The lungs get bigger.
[explaining] So air flows in, down the trachea and into the lungs.
[clear] Here is a chant for breathing in. Ribs up and out. Diaphragm down. Air in.
[encouraging] Say it with me. Ribs up and out. Diaphragm down. Air in.
[gentle] Hands on your ribs. Breathe in slowly. Feel your ribs move up and out.
[long pause]
## 10
[clear] Now let's breathe out. Breathing out is called exhalation.
[slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Ex. Ha. La. Tion. Four taps.
[long pause]
[explaining] Ex means out. In means in. Exhalation is out. Inhalation is in.
[explaining] To breathe out, the muscles relax.
[explaining] The ribs move down and in. The diaphragm rises back into a dome.
[explaining] The space in your chest gets smaller. The lungs get smaller too.
[explaining] So air is pushed out of the lungs.
[clear] The air moving in and out of the lungs is called ventilation.
[slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Ven. Ti. La. Tion. Four taps.
[long pause]
[encouraging] Breathe out slowly. Feel your ribs move down and in.
[long pause]
## 11
[warm] Back to our sports scientists. They measure breathing rate.
[explaining] Breathing rate is how many times you breathe in and out in one minute.
[explaining] Each breath in and out counts as one.
[curious] Here is a puzzle. Sam takes seven breaths in thirty seconds.
[curious] What is Sam's breathing rate for one minute?
[long pause]
[pleased] Fourteen breaths per minute! Thirty seconds is half a minute. Seven times two is fourteen.
[explaining] Exercise makes muscles work harder. They need more energy from respiration.
[explaining] So you breathe faster and deeper. That gets more oxygen to your cells.
[gentle] Sit still. Count your breaths while the timer runs.
[long pause]
