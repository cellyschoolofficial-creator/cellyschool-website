# 7Cb Muscles and blood + Scientific questions — voice script

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book p40–43. 140 lines, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts. Voice: Sulafat (Gemini Flash TTS). No sentence is longer than 15 words.

## Script

### Scene 0
- **Cb00_01** [curious, intriguing] Here is a mystery. Your heart beats about one hundred thousand times every day.
- **Cb00_02** [curious] It never stops for a rest. Not even when you sleep.
- **Cb00_03** [warm] So how does it keep your blood moving? Today, we solve the case of the busy pump.

### Scene 1
- **Cb01_01** [warm, smiling] Hello, scientist! Welcome back, detective.
- **Cb01_02** [calm, recalling] Last time, we found out how muscles help us breathe.
- **Cb01_03** [clear] What happens to oxygen in the lungs?
*(long pause)*
- **Cb01_04** [pleased] It goes into the blood. Carbon dioxide comes out.
- **Cb01_05** [clear] And which two muscles help us breathe in?
*(long pause)*
- **Cb01_06** [pleased] The intercostal muscles and the diaphragm.
- **Cb01_07** [explaining] The blood then carries oxygen to every cell in your body.
- **Cb01_08** [curious] But blood cannot move on its own. What pushes it? Another muscle!
- **Cb01_09** [gentle] Put your hand flat on the left side of your chest. Can you feel a beat?
*(long pause)*

### Scene 2
- **Cb02_01** [explaining] Each beat of your heart pushes blood into your arteries.
- **Cb02_02** [explaining] You can feel each push as a beat. This is called your pulse.
- **Cb02_03** [clear] The pulse rate is the number of beats in one minute.
- **Cb02_04** [explaining] Most people have a pulse rate between sixty and one hundred.
- **Cb02_05** [explaining] Many athletes have a resting pulse rate below fifty.
- **Cb02_06** [clear] Fitter people usually have a lower resting pulse rate.
- **Cb02_07** [explaining] Here is how to measure your pulse. Put two fingers on the inside of your wrist.
- **Cb02_08** [explaining] Count the beats for fifteen seconds. Then multiply by four.
- **Cb02_09** [curious] Hattie counts sixteen beats in fifteen seconds. What is her pulse rate?
*(long pause)*
- **Cb02_10** [pleased] Sixty-four beats per minute! Sixteen times four is sixty-four.
- **Cb02_11** [gentle] Find your pulse with two fingers. Count the beats while the timer runs.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 3
- **Cb03_01** [clear] The heart is an organ. It is a pump made of muscle.
- **Cb03_02** [explaining] Inside, it has spaces called chambers. The chambers fill with blood.
- **Cb03_03** [explaining] Then the muscle tissue in the wall contracts.
- **Cb03_04** [explaining] The chambers get smaller, and blood is pumped out of the heart.
- **Cb03_05** [explaining] Then the muscle relaxes, and the chambers fill up again.
- **Cb03_06** [clear] The left side of the heart has much more muscle than the right side.
- **Cb03_07** [curious] Why? The left side pumps blood all around the body.
- **Cb03_08** [explaining] The right side only pumps blood to the lungs. That is a much shorter trip.
- **Cb03_09** [explaining] More muscle means a stronger push.
- **Cb03_10** [gentle] Make a fist. Your heart is about this size. Squeeze it, then relax it, like a heartbeat.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 4
- **Cb04_01** [explaining] Blood travels in tubes called blood vessels. There are three kinds.
- **Cb04_02** [clear] The first kind is an artery.
- **Cb04_03** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Ar. Te. Ry. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cb04_04** [explaining] Arteries carry blood away from the heart. Here is a trick. Arteries go Away.
- **Cb04_05** [explaining] The blood in arteries is at high pressure. So arteries have thick, strong walls.
- **Cb04_06** [clear] The second kind is a capillary.
- **Cb04_07** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Cap. Il. Lar. Y. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cb04_08** [explaining] Capill means hair. Capillaries are tiny, as thin as hairs.
- **Cb04_09** [explaining] They have very thin walls. Oxygen and nutrients get out to the cells.
- **Cb04_10** [explaining] Waste, like carbon dioxide, gets in.
- **Cb04_11** [clear] The third kind is a vein. Veins carry blood back to the heart.
- **Cb04_12** [explaining] Veins are wider, with thinner walls. The blood moves more slowly, at low pressure.
- **Cb04_13** [explaining] Think of roads. Arteries are fast motorways. Capillaries are tiny lanes to every house.
- **Cb04_14** [explaining] And veins are wide, slow roads home.
- **Cb04_15** [encouraging] Say the trick with me. Arteries go Away.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 5
- **Cb05_01** [curious] Now, what is blood made of?
- **Cb05_02** [explaining] The liquid part of blood is called plasma.
- **Cb05_03** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Plas. Ma. Two taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cb05_04** [explaining] Plasma carries dissolved nutrients and waste, like carbon dioxide.
- **Cb05_05** [clear] Red blood cells carry oxygen.
- **Cb05_06** [explaining] They are packed with a red substance called haemoglobin. It carries the oxygen.
- **Cb05_07** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Hae. Mo. Glo. Bin. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cb05_08** [explaining] Haem means blood. Red blood cells have no nucleus. So there is more room for haemoglobin.
- **Cb05_09** [explaining] They are shaped like a disc, curved in on both sides.
- **Cb05_10** [explaining] This shape gives a large surface. So oxygen gets in and out quickly.
- **Cb05_11** [explaining] They are also flexible. They can squeeze through tiny capillaries.
- **Cb05_12** [clear] White blood cells fight infections. They attack microorganisms.
- **Cb05_13** [explaining] Both kinds of blood cell are made in the bone marrow, inside your bones.
- **Cb05_14** [gentle] Make a circle with your finger and thumb. Press the middle in. That is a red blood cell shape.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 6
- **Cb06_01** [playful] Thumbs up if it is true. Thumbs down if it is false.
- **Cb06_02** [clear] Number one. Arteries carry blood away from the heart.
*(long pause)*
- **Cb06_03** [pleased] Thumbs up! Arteries go Away.
- **Cb06_04** [clear] Number two. Capillaries have thick walls.
*(long pause)*
- **Cb06_05** [pleased] Thumbs down! Capillaries have very thin walls, so substances can get in and out.
- **Cb06_06** [clear] Number three. Red blood cells carry oxygen.
*(long pause)*
- **Cb06_07** [pleased] Thumbs up! They use haemoglobin to carry it.
- **Cb06_08** [warm, proud] Brilliant detective work. Part one is done. Time for a break!

### Scene 7
- **Cb07_01** [warm] Welcome back, detective. Now, let's follow the blood on its journey.
- **Cb07_02** [clear] Blood going round and round the body is called circulation.
- **Cb07_03** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Cir. Cu. La. Tion. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cb07_04** [explaining] Circ means circle. The heart, blood vessels and blood make the circulatory system.
- **Cb07_05** [explaining] First, the heart pumps blood to the lungs. The blood picks up oxygen.
- **Cb07_06** [explaining] The blood comes back to the heart. Then the heart pumps it to the body.
- **Cb07_07** [explaining] In the body, the cells take the oxygen. Then the blood goes back to the heart.
- **Cb07_08** [clear] So blood goes through the heart twice on each trip. This is a double circulatory system.
- **Cb07_09** [explaining] A fish is different. Its blood goes through the heart only once.
- **Cb07_10** [explaining] Cells use the oxygen and nutrients for respiration. They also use them to grow and repair.
- **Cb07_11** [gentle] Trace a figure of eight in the air. One loop for the lungs. One loop for the body.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 8
- **Cb08_01** [warm] Here is a true story about the heart.
- **Cb08_02** [explaining] Long ago, a Roman doctor called Galen had an idea about blood.
- **Cb08_03** [explaining] He said blood was made in the liver, and then used up by the body.
- **Cb08_04** [explaining] People believed Galen for about one thousand five hundred years.
- **Cb08_05** [curious] But nobody tested his idea. Was he right?
- **Cb08_06** [explaining] Then, about four hundred years ago, William Harvey had a different idea.
- **Cb08_07** [explaining] He thought the heart was a pump. He decided to test his idea.
- **Cb08_08** [clear] An idea that we can test is called a hypothesis.
- **Cb08_09** [explaining] Harvey worked out how much blood the heart pumps in one day.
- **Cb08_10** [explaining] It was far too much for the liver to make. So Galen must be wrong.
- **Cb08_11** [gentle] Tap your wrist like a detective's notebook. Test ideas. Do not just believe them.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 9
- **Cb09_01** [explaining] Harvey had a new idea. Blood does not get used up. It goes round and round.
- **Cb09_02** [explaining] It travels away from the heart in arteries. It comes back in veins.
- **Cb09_03** [curious] But how does blood get from the arteries to the veins?
- **Cb09_04** [explaining] Harvey made a prediction. There must be tiny tubes joining them.
- **Cb09_05** [explaining] Harvey could not see them. His eyes were not good enough.
- **Cb09_06** [explaining] Later, in sixteen sixty-one, Marcello Malpighi used a microscope.
- **Cb09_07** [explaining] He found the tiny tubes. They were capillaries!
- **Cb09_08** [warm] Harvey's prediction was correct. Testing ideas helps science move forward.
- **Cb09_09** [gentle] Hold your hands up like a microscope. Look closely for tiny tubes.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 10
- **Cb10_01** [clear] Harvey asked a scientific question. A scientific question can be tested.
- **Cb10_02** [explaining] It can be answered again and again, using experiments and investigations.
- **Cb10_03** [explaining] For example, does exercise change your pulse rate? We can test that.
- **Cb10_04** [explaining] Some scientific questions have no answer yet. We need new machines or more tests.
- **Cb10_05** [clear] Some questions are not scientific. We cannot test them.
- **Cb10_06** [explaining] For example, is red the best colour? There is no test for that.
- **Cb10_07** [clear] Some questions are ethical questions.
- **Cb10_08** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Eth. I. Cal. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cb10_09** [explaining] An ethical question is about what people think is fair, or right and wrong.
- **Cb10_10** [explaining] For example, should Harvey have used animals in his experiments?
- **Cb10_11** [explaining] People can have different answers. Science alone cannot decide.

### Scene 11
- **Cb11_01** [playful] Sorting game! Is each question scientific, or ethical?
- **Cb11_02** [clear] Number one. Do fit people have lower pulse rates?
*(long pause)*
- **Cb11_03** [pleased] Scientific! We can measure pulse rates and compare them.
- **Cb11_04** [clear] Number two. Is it fair to test medicines on animals?
*(long pause)*
- **Cb11_05** [pleased] Ethical! It is about right and wrong.
- **Cb11_06** [clear] Number three. Does running make your heart beat faster?
*(long pause)*
- **Cb11_07** [pleased] Scientific! You can test it with your own pulse.
- **Cb11_08** [explaining] Harvey also used maths. Each beat, the left side pumps about sixty-five cubic centimetres.
- **Cb11_09** [explaining] So blood pumped in one minute is sixty-five times the pulse rate.
- **Cb11_10** [gentle] March on the spot for a moment. Then feel your pulse again. Is it faster?
*(long pause)*

### Scene 12
- **Cb12_01** [upbeat, friendly] Quiz time! Say the answer out loud.
- **Cb12_02** [clear] Question one. How do you work out your pulse rate?
*(long pause)*
- **Cb12_03** [pleased] Count the beats for fifteen seconds. Then multiply by four.
- **Cb12_04** [clear] Question two. Which way do arteries carry blood?
*(long pause)*
- **Cb12_05** [pleased] Away from the heart.
- **Cb12_06** [clear] Question three. Why do capillaries have thin walls?
*(long pause)*
- **Cb12_07** [pleased] So oxygen and nutrients can get out to the cells.
- **Cb12_08** [clear] Question four. Why do red blood cells have no nucleus?
*(long pause)*
- **Cb12_09** [pleased] So there is more room for haemoglobin, to carry oxygen.
- **Cb12_10** [clear] Question five. Is this question scientific or ethical? Should we test medicines on animals?
*(long pause)*
- **Cb12_11** [pleased] Ethical.
- **Cb12_12** [warm, calm] Here are three big ideas.
- **Cb12_13** [warm, calm] One. The heart is a muscle that pumps blood. Arteries go Away. Veins come back.
- **Cb12_14** [warm, calm] Two. Red blood cells carry oxygen. White blood cells fight infections.
- **Cb12_15** [warm, calm] Three. Scientific questions can be tested. Ethical questions are about right and wrong.
- **Cb12_16** [warm] Case solved, detective! The heart is a busy muscle pump.
- **Cb12_17** [warm] Next time, we explore the skeleton.
- **Cb12_18** [warm, cheerful] Great work today. See you next time, scientist!
