## Script

### Scene 0
- **Cd00_01** [curious, intriguing] Here is a mystery. Your muscles can pull on your bones. But they can never push.
- **Cd00_02** [curious] So how do you straighten your arm again after you bend it?
- **Cd00_03** [warm] Today, we solve the case of the muscle that cannot push.

### Scene 1
- **Cd01_01** [warm, smiling] Hello, scientist! Welcome back, detective.
- **Cd01_02** [calm, recalling] Last time, we explored the skeleton.
- **Cd01_03** [clear] What are the three main jobs of the skeleton?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd01_04** [pleased] Support, protection and movement. Some People Move.
- **Cd01_05** [clear] And what joins a muscle to a bone?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd01_06** [pleased] A tendon.
- **Cd01_07** [curious] Today, we look at the third job. How do muscles move your bones?
- **Cd01_08** [gentle] Wiggle your fingers. Then wiggle your toes. Muscles are moving bones right now.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 2
- **Cd02_01** [clear] Your muscles and bones work together. They form an organ system.
- **Cd02_02** [clear] It is called the locomotor system.
- **Cd02_03** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Lo. Co. Mo. Tor. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cd02_04** [explaining] Locomotion means moving. This system lets you move every part of your body.
- **Cd02_05** [clear] Can you name two organs in the locomotor system?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd02_06** [pleased] Bones and muscles! Each bone is an organ. Each muscle is an organ too.
- **Cd02_07** [explaining] The study of how muscles and bones work together is called biomechanics.
- **Cd02_08** [gentle] Stand up and march on the spot. Your locomotor system is working.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 3
- **Cd03_01** [curious] Here is a surprise. Your skeleton is always changing.
- **Cd03_02** [explaining] When athletes train, their muscles get bigger. Their bones change too.
- **Cd03_03** [explaining] Bigger muscles pull harder. So bones grow thicker and stronger, to support them.
- **Cd03_04** [explaining] People who do lots of hard work with their hands get thicker arm bones.
- **Cd03_05** [curious] A boxer trains for a few years. Now he is heavier. Why?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd03_06** [pleased] His muscles and bones have grown bigger.
- **Cd03_07** [explaining] An X-ray shows a tennis player's arms. The right arm bones are thicker.
- **Cd03_08** [curious] Which hand do you think holds the racquet?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd03_09** [pleased] The right hand! That arm works harder, so its bones grew thicker.
- **Cd03_10** [explaining] Runners have stronger leg bones than cyclists. Running hits the bones harder.
- **Cd03_11** [explaining] Muscles and bones can also mend if they are torn or broken.
- **Cd03_12** [gentle] Make a strong fist. Feel your forearm. Can you feel it get hard?
*(long pause)*

### Scene 4
- **Cd04_01** [explaining] The muscles that close your fingers are in your forearm, near your elbow.
- **Cd04_02** [clear] When you made a fist, those muscles contracted.
- **Cd04_03** [explaining] Contract means a muscle gets shorter and fatter.
- **Cd04_04** [explaining] A muscle is joined to a bone. So when it contracts, it pulls on the bone.
- **Cd04_05** [clear] That pull is a force. We measure it in newtons, using a force meter.
- **Cd04_06** [clear] Your strongest muscle is your jaw muscle. It is called the masseter.
- **Cd04_07** [explaining] A human bite is about two thousand five hundred newtons. A dog's is much stronger.
- **Cd04_08** [playful] An alligator bites with about nine thousand five hundred newtons!
- **Cd04_09** [explaining] Your smallest muscle is in your ear. It is only about one millimetre long.
- **Cd04_10** [gentle] Bite your teeth together gently. Feel your jaw muscle bulge by your cheek.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 5
- **Cd05_01** [clear] When a muscle stops contracting, it relaxes.
- **Cd05_02** [explaining] It goes back to its first size and shape.
- **Cd05_03** [explaining] A relaxed muscle makes no force. It cannot push the bone back.
- **Cd05_04** [clear] So muscles can only pull. They can never push.
- **Cd05_05** [explaining] Here is a trick to remember it. Muscles pull. Never push.
- **Cd05_06** [encouraging] Say it with me. Muscles pull. Never push.
- **Cd05_07** [curious] So how does a bone move back again? That is our next clue.
- **Cd05_08** [gentle] Bend your arm slowly. Feel the front of your upper arm get hard.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 6
- **Cd06_01** [playful] Thumbs up if it is true. Thumbs down if it is false.
- **Cd06_02** [clear] Number one. Muscles get shorter and fatter when they contract.
*(long pause)*
- **Cd06_03** [pleased] Thumbs up!
- **Cd06_04** [clear] Number two. Muscles can push bones.
*(long pause)*
- **Cd06_05** [pleased] Thumbs down! Muscles can only pull.
- **Cd06_06** [clear] Number three. Training can make your bones thicker.
*(long pause)*
- **Cd06_07** [pleased] Thumbs up! Thicker bones support bigger muscles.
- **Cd06_08** [warm, proud] Brilliant detective work. Part one is done. Time for a break!

### Scene 7
- **Cd07_01** [warm] Welcome back, detective. Let's crack the case.
- **Cd07_02** [explaining] A bone in a joint often moves in two directions. Up and down.
- **Cd07_03** [explaining] Each muscle can only pull one way. So the bone needs two muscles.
- **Cd07_04** [clear] One muscle pulls it one way. The other muscle pulls it back.
- **Cd07_05** [clear] Two muscles that work like this are called an antagonistic pair.
- **Cd07_06** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] An. Tag. O. Nis. Tic. Five taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cd07_07** [explaining] Antagonistic means working against each other. They pull in opposite directions.
- **Cd07_08** [curious] So why do muscles work in pairs?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd07_09** [pleased] Because muscles can only pull. They cannot push.
- **Cd07_10** [explaining] You can make a model pair with card and string.
- **Cd07_11** [explaining] But string cannot contract like a real muscle. So the model is not perfect.
- **Cd07_12** [gentle] Hook your fingers together. Pull one way, then the other way.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 8
- **Cd08_01** [clear] Your upper arm has an antagonistic pair. The biceps and the triceps.
- **Cd08_02** [explaining] The biceps is at the front. The triceps is at the back.
- **Cd08_03** [clear] To lift your lower arm, the biceps contracts. The triceps is stretched.
- **Cd08_04** [clear] To put your arm down, the triceps contracts. The biceps is stretched.
- **Cd08_05** [curious] So when the biceps contracts, what happens to the triceps?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd08_06** [pleased] It relaxes, and the biceps stretches it out.
- **Cd08_07** [explaining] That stretch gets the relaxed muscle ready to contract again.
- **Cd08_08** [explaining] Here is a trick. Biceps bends. Triceps straightens.
- **Cd08_09** [explaining] There are other pairs too. The wrist flexor and wrist extensor move your wrist.
- **Cd08_10** [explaining] In your thigh, the quadriceps and the biceps femoris, or hamstring, move your lower leg.
- **Cd08_11** [explaining] In your lower leg, the calf muscle and the shin muscle move your foot.
- **Cd08_12** [curious] Point your toes to the ground. Which muscle contracts?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd08_13** [pleased] The calf muscle!
- **Cd08_14** [curious] Now raise your toes up. Which muscle contracts?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd08_15** [pleased] The shin muscle!
- **Cd08_16** [explaining] Your jaw has a pair too. The masseter closes it. Another muscle opens it.
- **Cd08_17** [gentle] Put a hand on your upper arm. Bend and straighten. Feel the two muscles swap.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 9
- **Cd09_01** [curious] But how does a muscle know when to contract? The brain tells it.
- **Cd09_02** [explaining] The brain sends electrical messages down the spinal cord, then along nerves to the muscle.
- **Cd09_03** [clear] These electrical messages are called impulses.
- **Cd09_04** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Im. Pulse. Two taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cd09_05** [explaining] Here is the path. Brain, then spinal cord, then nerve, then muscle. Then it contracts!
- **Cd09_06** [explaining] Muscles work hard, so their cells need lots of energy.
- **Cd09_07** [explaining] Respiration releases energy. It happens in tiny parts of cells called mitochondria.
- **Cd09_08** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Mi. To. Chon. Dri. A. Five taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cd09_09** [explaining] We say my-toe-kon-dree-uh. Muscle cells have more mitochondria than most other cells.
- **Cd09_10** [gentle] Kick one leg forward slowly. Brain, spinal cord, nerve, muscle!
*(long pause)*

### Scene 10
- **Cd10_01** [explaining] Sometimes, parts of the locomotor system get worn or lost.
- **Cd10_02** [explaining] Ligaments hold the bones in a joint together.
- **Cd10_03** [explaining] Cartilage and a slippery liquid help joints move easily. They stop bones rubbing.
- **Cd10_04** [explaining] If a joint wears out, moving it hurts. A hip can be replaced with a new joint.
- **Cd10_05** [explaining] Some people lose an arm or a leg. They can get an artificial one.
- **Cd10_06** [clear] An artificial body part is called a prosthesis.
- **Cd10_07** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Pros. The. Sis. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Cd10_08** [explaining] A good artificial leg must be strong, light and comfortable. It must let you move.
- **Cd10_09** [explaining] One artificial arm has a gripper. A spring holds it shut.
- **Cd10_10** [explaining] The person moves their shoulder back. This pulls a cord, and the gripper opens.
- **Cd10_11** [explaining] A prosthetist makes and tests each one. They score it against a list called criteria.
- **Cd10_12** [explaining] Then they change it to fit better. New three-D printing makes this faster.
- **Cd10_13** [gentle] Pinch your thumb and finger together. Imagine doing it with no thumb.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 11
- **Cd11_01** [playful] Detective game! Which muscle contracts? Biceps, triceps, calf or quadriceps?
- **Cd11_02** [clear] Number one. You lift a cup to your mouth.
*(long pause)*
- **Cd11_03** [pleased] The biceps! Biceps bends.
- **Cd11_04** [clear] Number two. You straighten your arm to throw a ball.
*(long pause)*
- **Cd11_05** [pleased] The triceps! Triceps straightens.
- **Cd11_06** [clear] Number three. You stand on tiptoe.
*(long pause)*
- **Cd11_07** [pleased] The calf muscle! It points your toes down.
- **Cd11_08** [clear] Number four. You straighten your leg to kick a ball.
*(long pause)*
- **Cd11_09** [pleased] The quadriceps! Its partner, the hamstring, is stretched.
- **Cd11_10** [clear] Bonus question. Which part of the body sends the impulse to the muscle?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd11_11** [pleased] The brain! It goes down the spinal cord and along a nerve.

### Scene 12
- **Cd12_01** [upbeat, friendly] Quiz time! Say the answer out loud.
- **Cd12_02** [clear] Question one. Which two kinds of organ make the locomotor system?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd12_03** [pleased] Bones and muscles.
- **Cd12_04** [clear] Question two. What happens to a muscle when it contracts?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd12_05** [pleased] It gets shorter and fatter, and it pulls on a bone.
- **Cd12_06** [clear] Question three. Why do muscles work in antagonistic pairs?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd12_07** [pleased] Because muscles can only pull. They cannot push.
- **Cd12_08** [clear] Question four. What carries impulses from the brain to a muscle?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd12_09** [pleased] The spinal cord, then nerves.
- **Cd12_10** [clear] Question five. Where in a cell is energy released?
*(long pause)*
- **Cd12_11** [pleased] In the mitochondria.
- **Cd12_12** [warm, calm] Here are three big ideas.
- **Cd12_13** [warm, calm] One. Muscles and bones form the locomotor system. Training makes both grow.
- **Cd12_14** [warm, calm] Two. Muscles only pull. So they work in antagonistic pairs, like biceps and triceps.
- **Cd12_15** [warm, calm] Three. Impulses from the brain make muscles contract. Mitochondria give them energy.
- **Cd12_16** [warm] Case solved, detective! The triceps pulls your arm straight. Muscles pull, never push.
- **Cd12_17** [warm] Next time, we find out how drugs change the way your body works.
- **Cd12_18** [warm, cheerful] Great work today. See you next time, scientist!
