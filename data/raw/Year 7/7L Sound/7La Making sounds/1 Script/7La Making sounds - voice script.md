# 7La Making sounds — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Hummingbird wing beats** (La03, La07): the book says a giant hummingbird flaps about 12 times a second, and bee hummingbirds can flap over 80 times a second (SB p183 Q4). The script says "about twelve" and "more than eighty".
- ⚑ **Ernst Chladni** (true story, La10) is ◇, beyond the book. He published his sand figures in 1787. The script says "over two hundred years ago" and gives no date or Napoleon detail.
- ⚑ **Guitar answers** (La08): the book asks how to make high, low, loud and quiet notes on a guitar (SB p181 Q4) but gives no answer. "Shorter or tighter string = higher note" and "pluck harder = louder" are the standard answers ◇.
- **"Usually":** the book says smaller objects "generally" vibrate faster and make higher notes. The script says "usually" and adds "a real scientist would test it" (Henna's question, SB p183 Q6).
- **Book facts used:** sounds are made by something vibrating (moving backwards and forwards); people and animals use sound to communicate, warn of danger, win a mate, hunt; leopard roars to warn predators; hummingbirds hum because their wings flap fast; bats use sound to find their way; intensity (volume) and pitch; smaller objects generally vibrate faster and make higher notes; frequency = vibrations per second, in hertz (Hz); a bell vibrating 400 times a second = 400 Hz; amplitude = size of a vibration, bigger amplitude = louder; a tuning fork tapped harder is louder; short tubular bells are higher than long ones; vocal folds across the windpipe; grasshoppers scrape toothed legs on a wing; gorillas thump chests or the ground; some whale calls are louder than jet engines and heard over 800 km away; a tuning fork vibrating 500 times a second (SB p182 Q3).
- **Safety (home lab):** ruler twang on a table and a rubber-band tissue box. Keep loud sounds away from ears, never put anything in an ear.
- ◇ **"No wobble, no sound"**, **"High and fast. Low and slow."** and **"Big is loud. Small is soft."** are Volty-made tricks.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7La Making sounds (p181–183). Celly School's first lesson of 7L Sound.
- **Size:** 139 spoken lines (Volty 129, Bolt 10), 24 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1340 words. That is roughly 16–17 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice Autonoe).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ia.

**World:** The Particle Stadium. When Volty says "Specs on", the air, water and walls turn into stadiums full of tiny green particle fans, each in their own seat. Today the Stadium Band warms up: a guitar, tubular bells, a drum and a hummingbird. Above the stage hangs the Vibe-o-meter with two dials: HOW FAST (frequency, pitch) and HOW BIG (amplitude, loudness).
- **Case:** "The case of the shared secret" (the 7Ke teaser).
- **Answered at the end:** every sound, from a hummingbird's hum to your voice, is made by something vibrating.

**Main tricks:**
- "No wobble, no sound." Hand on throat, hum, feel the buzz.
- "High and fast. Low and slow." Flutter fingers fast above the head, then wave slowly by the knees.
- "Big is loud. Small is soft." Arms stretched wide, then tiny hands.
