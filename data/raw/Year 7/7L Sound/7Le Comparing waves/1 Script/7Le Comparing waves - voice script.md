# 7Le Comparing waves — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **The Draupner wave** (true story, Le10) is ◇, beyond the book. On 1 January 1995, instruments on the Draupner gas platform in the North Sea recorded a single wave about 25.6 metres high, around twice the height of the waves around it. The script says "about twenty-five metres" and "one key explanation", because the causes of rogue waves are still researched.
- ⚑ **Whales and sonar** (Le08): the book says strandings "may" happen because of human-made underwater noise (SB p196). The script keeps "may".
- ⚑ **Unit and Year 7 finale** (Le12): the five-idea recap and the Year 7 send-off are Celly School additions. The Year 7 list of units follows the book's contents (7A–7L).
- **Book facts used:** ripples are small waves spreading out from a duck, transferring energy; water particles move up and down but the water as a whole doesn't move outwards, so other ducks only bob; sound is a longitudinal wave, the particles vibrate in the same direction as the wave travels; water waves are transverse, the particles move at right angles to the direction of travel; crest and trough; amplitude of a transverse wave is the maximum distance up or down; amplitude gets smaller further from the source because the energy spreads out; waves reflect off solid barriers like canal sides and harbour walls; waves can pass through each other, adding together or cancelling out (superposition); two crests or two troughs make a bigger wave, a crest and a trough cancel out; waves near cliffs can be bigger and come from more than one direction; noise affects animals: city songbirds sing earlier, louder or at different frequencies; some bats find it hard to hunt in noise; helicopter safaris frighten animals; naval sonar reaches dolphins and divers, dolphins can hear it but divers can't.
- **Safety (home lab):** the bath or tray wave lab needs a grown-up watching at all times around water. Mop up spills.
- ◇ **"Longitudinal goes along. Transverse goes across."**, **"Crest on crest makes a giant. Crest on trough makes it flat."** and **"Ducks don't drift. They bob."** are Volty-made tricks.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Le Comparing waves (p194–196), including the page on how human noises affect animals. Celly School's last lesson of 7L Sound, and the last lesson of Year 7.
- **Size:** 129 spoken lines (Volty 120, Bolt 9), 20 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1320 words. That is roughly 15–16 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice Autonoe).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ia, with a longer final scene: the 7L recap, then the Year 7 finale. No next-lesson tease.

**World:** The Grand Finale at the Particle Stadium, next to a calm duck pond. The crowd does two waves. The shoulder-nudge (fans rock along the row, the same direction as the wave) is longitudinal, like sound. The stand-up wave (fans stand and sit, while the wave goes sideways) is transverse, like ripples on water. When two stand-up waves meet, the fans jump extra high. When a stand-up meets a crouch, the row goes flat for a moment.
- **Case:** "The case of the bobbing duck" (the 7Ld teaser).
- **Answered at the end:** waves carry energy, not water. The water particles only move up and down, so the duck bobs in the same place while the ripples race past.

**Main tricks:**
- "Longitudinal goes along. Transverse goes across." Push an arm forwards along a line, then chop a hand up and down across it.
- "Crest on crest makes a giant. Crest on trough makes it flat." Stack two fists high, then meet one fist up with one fist down.
- "Ducks don't drift. They bob." Bob a hand up and down on the spot.
