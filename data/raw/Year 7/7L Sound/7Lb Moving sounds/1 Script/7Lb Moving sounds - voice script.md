# 7Lb Moving sounds — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Speed of sound figures** (Lb05): taken from the book's table D (SB p185), read from the page image: air (20 °C) 343 m/s, water (10 °C) 1450 m/s, wood 3600 m/s, glass 3950 m/s, steel 6100 m/s. The script uses air, water and steel only. The design doc's "water 1500 m/s" is NOT the book's figure, so it is not used.
- ⚑ **Robert Boyle's bell jar** (true story, Lb10) is ◇, beyond the book. Boyle's 1660 air-pump experiments with Robert Hooke as his assistant are well documented. They could not remove all the air, so the script says "almost silent".
- ⚑ **"Space is almost empty"** (Lb02_08, Lb12_14): the book says sound cannot travel through the empty space between the astronauts. The script says space is "almost empty", "nearly a vacuum" ◇, which is more accurate.
- **Oscilloscope example** (Lb08): the book's trace B: one wave takes 0.2 seconds, so 5 waves pass each second, frequency 5 Hz (SB p186). Read as "nought point two seconds".
- **Scatter graph** (Lb08): the book's graph D shows the speed of sound in air rising with temperature, a positive correlation (SB p186–187). No numbers from the graph are used.
- **Book facts used:** astronauts use radios because sound cannot travel through empty space; sound needs a medium (solid, liquid or gas) and cannot travel through a vacuum; a loudspeaker makes air particles move backwards and forwards and pass on the vibration; squashed regions = higher pressure, spread-out regions = lower pressure, so sound waves are pressure waves; amplitude = distance moved by the particles, louder = bigger; frequency = waves passing per second; sound waves transfer energy, not particles, louder sounds transfer more energy; touching helmets lets astronauts talk; sound travels faster in solids than liquids, and faster in liquids than gases, because vibrations pass on more easily when particles are closer; sound spreads out in three directions and gets quieter; cupped hands or a paper cone help your voice carry; the slinky model and its limits; oscilloscope traces; scatter graphs, lines of best fit, positive and negative correlation.
- **Safety (home lab):** cup telephone (a grown-up makes the holes) and an ear on the table with gentle taps. Whispers only, never shout into a cup, never put anything in an ear.
- ◇ **"No particles, no party"**, **"The energy travels. The particles stay."** and **"Steel speeds. Water wades. Air ambles."** are Volty-made tricks.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Lb Moving sounds (p184–187), including the Working Scientifically pages on line graphs and scatter graphs. Celly School's second lesson of 7L Sound.
- **Size:** 133 spoken lines (Volty 124, Bolt 9), 21 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1330 words. That is roughly 16–17 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice Autonoe).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ia.

**World:** The Particle Stadium on tour. A drummer stands at one end of each stadium. When the drum thumps, the first green fan nudges the next, and the nudge passes along the row. One fan wears a red hat, so we can see she only rocks back and forth and never leaves her seat. Squashed-up fans light up as HIGH PRESSURE, spread-out fans as LOW PRESSURE. There are four stadiums: Solid (steel), Liquid (water), Gas (air) and the Empty Stadium (space), where the drum thumps and nothing happens.
- **Case:** "The case of the silent explosion" (the 7La teaser).
- **Answered at the end:** space is almost empty, nearly a vacuum. With no particles to pass on the vibrations, sound cannot travel, so a real explosion in space would be silent.

**Main tricks:**
- "No particles, no party." Hold out empty hands and shrug.
- "The energy travels. The particles stay." Rock gently forwards and back in your seat.
- "Steel speeds. Water wades. Air ambles." Zoom a hand fast, swim it, then stroll it slowly.

### Scene 4 · Energy on the move
- **Lb04_01** [clear] Sound waves transfer energy from one place to another.
