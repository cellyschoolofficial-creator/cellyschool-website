# 7Lc Detecting sounds — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Dame Evelyn Glennie** (hook, true story Lc10) is ◇, beyond the book. She is a Scottish solo percussionist who became profoundly deaf at about the age of 12, and often performs barefoot to feel the vibrations through the floor. The script says "profoundly deaf" and "feels the vibrations", never "hears nothing". It gives no other details.
- ⚑ **Hearing ranges** (Lc07): read from the book's graph E (SB p189): human 20–20 000 Hz, dog 67–45 000 Hz, bat 2000–110 000 Hz, elephant 16–12 000 Hz, dolphin 75–150 000 Hz. The script uses human, elephant and bat (bat as "far above") with no other numbers.
- ⚑ **Ear bones** (Lc02): the book says "tiny bones". The names hammer, anvil and stirrup ◇ are not used.
- **Decibels** (Lc05): the book says 0 dB is the quietest sound humans can hear, normal conversation is about 60 dB, sounds are painful at above about 130 dB, and some countries make employers provide hearing protection above 85 dB (SB p189).
- **Book facts used:** most animals use ears to detect sound waves; the outer ear channels sound in; the six steps (ear canal, eardrum vibrates, tiny bones amplify, liquid in the cochlea, tiny hairs make impulses, auditory nerve to the brain); you hear when impulses reach the brain; loud sounds can damage ears, including headphones; ear protection; soft materials like carpets and curtains absorb sound; owls hear much quieter sounds than humans; microphones have a diaphragm that vibrates and circuits that change the vibrations into changes in current; sound intensity meters use decibels (dB); the auditory range of humans is 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz; infrasound and ultrasound; other animals have different auditory ranges; sheepdog whistles humans can't hear.
- **Safety (home lab):** a grown-up uses a phone sound-meter app; keep the volume low; never hold a phone or speaker against an ear; never put anything in an ear.
- ◇ **"Can Every Bat Catch Night Bugs?"** (canal, eardrum, bones, cochlea, nerve, brain), **"Infra is under. Ultra is over."** and **"Eighty-five? Protect your ears."** are Volty-made tricks. The first is from the design kit.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Lc Detecting sounds (p188–189). Celly School's third lesson of 7L Sound.
- **Size:** 127 spoken lines (Volty 117, Bolt 10), 21 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1220 words. That is roughly 15–16 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice Autonoe).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ia.

**World:** The Ear Tunnel ride in the Particle Stadium. Volty rides a sound wave into a giant ear: down the ear canal, bump into the trampoline-like eardrum, through a relay of three tiny bones, into the snail-shell cochlea full of waving hairs, and onto the impulse train racing up the auditory nerve to Brain Station. Next to the ride stands the Decibel Tower, with one floor for each loudness.
- **Case:** "The case of the barefoot drummer" (the 7Lb teaser).
- **Answered at the end:** sound is vibration. The drummer feels the vibrations through the floor and her body. Our ears are super-sensitive vibration detectors that turn vibrations into impulses for the brain.

**Main tricks:**
- "Can Every Bat Catch Night Bugs?" Canal, Eardrum, Bones, Cochlea, Nerve, Brain. Six finger taps walking up the arm, from the wrist to the head.
- "Infra is under. Ultra is over." Point down, then point up.
- "Eighty-five? Protect your ears." Cup both hands over the ears.
