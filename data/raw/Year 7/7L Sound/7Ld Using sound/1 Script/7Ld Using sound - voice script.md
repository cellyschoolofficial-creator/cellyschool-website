# 7Ld Using sound — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Bats and Spallanzani** (true story, Ld10) are ◇, beyond the book. In the 1790s Lazzaro Spallanzani found bats could fly through obstacles in the dark, even with their eyes covered. Louis Jurine showed that bats with blocked ears crashed. In 1938 Donald Griffin used a special detector to show bats make ultrasound, and he later named echolocation. The script says "covered their eyes" and "blocked their ears", which is honest without being upsetting.
- ⚑ **"There and back"** (Ld05): the book says animals detect how far away an object is from the time the echo takes to return. The idea that the sound travels there and back, so the trip is twice the distance, is ◇. No sonar depth sums are given.
- **Lucas Murray** (Ld06) is in the book (SB p191 photo F): he is blind and uses clicks to play basketball and find his way around.
- **Tiger moths** (Ld07) are in the book (SB p191 Q6): they don't use echolocation but can detect and produce ultrasound. The script says they "may" confuse bats, because the book only asks the child to suggest how it helps.
- **Book facts used:** we use sound to communicate; telephones change sound waves to electrical signals and back; animals use warning calls; sound energy can be transmitted, absorbed or reflected; ultrasound can treat injuries (soften scar tissue) and clean jewellery and surgical instruments with tiny bubbles; an echo is a reflected sound; bats and dolphins use echolocation with ultrasound; the echo time tells how far away; they can sense direction; dolphins use sound because it travels through water much better than light; sonar finds fish, the depth of the sea and submarines; two echoes from one pulse; each bat can use different frequencies; acoustics; acoustic engineers; anechoic chambers with soft pointed shapes; the Dubai Fountain; noise barriers: hard materials reflect noise, earth banks and wooden fences absorb it; curved concert hall walls help stop echoes.
- **Safety (home lab):** the echo hunt is with a grown-up, in an open space away from roads. The bat-and-moth game is on a soft, clear floor, the "bat" walks slowly, and a grown-up is the spotter.
- ◇ **"Send. Smack. Sense."**, **"Bounce, soak or pass"** and **"Ultra means higher, not louder"** are Volty-made tricks.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Ld Using sound (p190–193), including the STEM pages on acoustic engineers. Celly School's fourth lesson of 7L Sound.
- **Size:** 129 spoken lines (Volty 120, Bolt 9), 20 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1300 words. That is roughly 15–16 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice Autonoe).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ia.

**World:** Echo Canyon, next to the Particle Stadium. When Volty shouts, the green fans pass the nudge across the canyon. It bounces off the cliff and races back, and a giant stopwatch shows the round trip. Bats, dolphins and a sonar ship all play the same game: send, bounce, time it.
- **Case:** "The case of the bat in the dark" (the 7Lc teaser).
- **Answered at the end:** the bat uses echolocation. It sends out bursts of ultrasound and listens for the echoes. The time the echo takes tells it how far away the wall or the moth is.

**Main tricks:**
- "Send. Smack. Sense." Shoot a hand out, slap the air like a wall, bring the hand back to your ear.
- "Bounce, soak or pass." Reflected, absorbed or transmitted: bounce a ball, squeeze a sponge, wave a hand through.
- "Ultra means higher, not louder." Point up, then shake your head at a big loud gesture.
