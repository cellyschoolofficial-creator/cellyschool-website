# 7Fb Indicators — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Robert Boyle, 1664:** the script says he "made the test famous". It never says he discovered or invented it, because plant-colour tests are older. Litmus comes from lichens (book), which had long been used as dyes.
- ⚑ **"Alkalis can be more dangerous than acids":**
  - The book says "in some ways they are more dangerous, especially if you get them in your eyes" (p90). RSC research (Kind, *Beyond Appearances*) adds that dilute alkalis are more corrosive than dilute acids.
  - The script keeps to the book's wording. The animation (Fb04_09–11) shows it on a cartoon skin and eye only. It is never a home activity.
- ⚑ **The book's photo A (p90) puts bleach into red cabbage indicator.**
  - The script and the home lab never use bleach.
  - The cabbage colours are shown roughly, because they vary with the cabbage and how strong the juice is.
- ◇ **Lake Van** (book Fact) is used as Bolt's joke and a wow line.
- ◇ **Ancient Arabia:** the book says "in ancient Arabia… *al quali*". The script follows the book, and Bolt's joke picks up the 7Ee alembic/alkali link.
- **"Acid makes it Red. Alkali makes it Blue."** This adapts the kit's "Base makes it Blue": bases are taught in 7Fe, so "alkali" is used here.
- ⚑ **Phenolphthalein** is said as "feen-ol-fthay-leen" (the book's guide). Record a test take first.
- **Home lab:** a grown-up does the knife and the hot water. Kitchen-safe items only, and no one drinks the test cups.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Fb Indicators (p90–91). Celly School's second lesson of unit 7F.
- **Size:** 160 spoken lines (Atomy 144, Bolt 16), 38 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1640 words. That is roughly 20 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Atomy the atom (voice Zephyr).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Fa.

**World:** the Rainbow Staircase. The indicators are the Chameleon Spies, who change their disguise colour on acid steps and alkali steps:
- Agent Litmus (a paper strip in a trench coat)
- Agent Cabbage
- Agent Methyl Orange
- Agent Phenolphthalein

Captain Caution guards the stairs.
- **Case:** "The case of the purple spy" (the 7Fa teaser: "A purple vegetable becomes a spy. Can it spot an acid?").
- **Answered at the end:** red cabbage juice is an indicator. It turns red in acids and blue-green in alkalis, but it cannot say how strong.

**Main tricks:**
- "Acid makes it Red. Alkali makes it Blue." (red card low in the left hand, blue card high in the right)
- "Al-kali = Ashes." (rub hands as if washing with soap)
- "Phenolphthalein Pinks in alkali. Plain in acid." (pinch cheeks)
- "Neutral is Not always safe." (Captain Caution's finger wag)
- Recall from 7Fa: "Corrosive Chews. Irritant Itches."

- **Fb00_04** [warm] Today, we solve the case of the purple spy.

### Scene 2 · What is an indicator? (the pickled cabbage)

- **Fb02_02** [curious] Now we mix in some soap. What do you think will happen?
*(long pause)*
- **Fb02_03 BOLT** [BOLT: confident, robotic] Nothing! A cabbage is a vegetable. Vegetables can't do chemistry.
- **Fb02_04** [explaining] Watch. Whoosh! The juice turns blue.
- **Fb02_05** [explaining] Now we add fizzy lemonade. Whoosh! It turns red again.
- **Fb02_06 BOLT** [BOLT: surprised, robotic] A colour-changing cabbage! Rebooting!
- **Fb02_07** [clear] Colours from plants that change like this are called indicators.
- **Fb02_08** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] In. Di. Ca. Tor. Four taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Fb02_09** [explaining] An indicator changes colour in acids and alkalis. It is a chemistry spy.
- **Fb02_10** [curious] To make the juice, the leaves are crushed with water. How do we separate the juice?
*(long pause)*
- **Fb02_11** [pleased] Filter it! Residue Rests. Filtrate Flows. The purple juice flows through.
- **Fb02_12** [warm] On our staircase, the indicators are the Chameleon Spies.
- **Fb02_13** [explaining] They change their disguise colour, depending on where they stand.
- **Fb02_14** [gentle] Pretend to put on a spy hat. Now change colour! Wiggle your fingers.
*(long pause)*

### Scene 3 · Agent Litmus: testing for acids
- **Fb03_01** [clear] Meet our first spy. Agent Litmus. Litmus is another indicator.
- **Fb03_02** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Lit. Mus. Two taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Fb03_03** [explaining] Litmus is made from a lichen. A lichen is a crusty living thing on rocks.
- **Fb03_04** [explaining] The word comes from lit-moss. Long ago, that meant coloured moss.
- **Fb03_05** [explaining] Litmus can be a liquid, called a solution. Or it can be soaked into paper.
- **Fb03_06** [curious] Agent Litmus steps onto vinegar. Vinegar is an acid. What colour will it turn?
*(long pause)*
- **Fb03_07** [pleased] Red! Most acids, like vinegar and fruit juices, turn litmus red.
- **Fb03_08** [explaining] Then Agent Litmus steps onto soap. Soap is an alkali. Whoosh! Blue.
- **Fb03_09** [explaining] Litmus can also be purple. That is blue and red together.
- **Fb03_10** [explaining] Litmus paper comes in two colours. Red litmus paper, and blue litmus paper.
- **Fb03_11** [explaining] Acids turn blue litmus paper red. Alkalis turn red litmus paper blue.
- **Fb03_12** [explaining] And neutral solutions leave both papers just as they were.
- **Fb03_13** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Acid makes it Red. Alkali makes it Blue.
- **Fb03_14** [gentle] Hold up your left hand low. Red! Hold up your right hand high. Blue!
*(long pause)*

### Scene 4 · Alkalis: ashes and soap (Bolt: alkalis are safe)
- **Fb04_01** [clear] Soap is an alkali. Alkalis feel soapy.
- **Fb04_02** [slow, rhythmic, one beat at a time] Al. Ka. Li. Three taps.
*(long pause)*
- **Fb04_03** [explaining] Long ago, in ancient Arabia, people took ashes from fires and mixed them with water.
- **Fb04_04** [explaining] They boiled it with animal fat. It made the very first soap.
- **Fb04_05** [explaining] In Arabic, the ashes were called al quali. That gave us the word alkali.
- **Fb04_06** [encouraging] Here is a trick. Al-kali means Ashes. Rub your hands, like washing with soap.
*(long pause)*
- **Fb04_07 BOLT** [BOLT: very sure, robotic] Alkalis make soap. Soap is gentle. So alkalis are safe!
- **Fb04_08** [curious] Hmm. Is that true? Remember drain cleaner?
*(long pause)*
- **Fb04_09** [explaining] Many alkalis are corrosive. They are too dangerous to feel.
- **Fb04_10** [explaining] They attack the natural oils in your skin. Your skin can start to turn into soap!
- **Fb04_11** [clear] In some ways, alkalis are more dangerous than acids. Especially in your eyes.
- **Fb04_12** [explaining] Alkalis can be irritants, harmful or corrosive. Just like acids.
- **Fb04_13 BOLT** [BOLT: shocked, robotic] Skin into soap? My safe-alkali file is deleted! Rebooting!
- **Fb04_14** [explaining] Oven cleaners use this. Their alkali attacks the grease, and turns it into soap.
- **Fb04_15** [explaining] Many household cleaners are alkaline. So Captain Caution watches them closely.

### Scene 5 · Neutral, and Neutral is Not always safe (Bolt: neutral means safe)
