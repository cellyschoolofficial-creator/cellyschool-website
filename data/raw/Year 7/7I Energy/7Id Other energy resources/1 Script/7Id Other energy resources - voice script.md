# 7Id Other energy resources — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Host:** Volty the spark (physics hero), with Bolt as the sidekick. Volty's voice isn't chosen yet (see 7Ia).
- ⚑ **Becquerel and Blyth** (true story, Id10): this goes beyond the book ◇.
  - In 1839, 19-year-old Edmond Becquerel found that light shining on certain materials made a small electric current. Solar cells use this effect.
  - In 1887, James Blyth built a wind turbine at his holiday cottage in Marykirk, Scotland. It charged batteries to light the cottage, and is often named as the first wind turbine to make electricity. The script says "often called".
  - The story that villagers called electricity "the work of the devil" is hard to confirm, so it is left out.
- ⚑ **Wind and the Sun:** the book says energy from the Sun causes wind and waves. The script adds one simple line ◇: the Sun warms the land unevenly, and air moves.
- ⚑ **Tides and the Moon:** the book lists tidal power as not depending on the Sun. "The Moon pulls the tides" is ◇ and kept to one line.
- ⚑ **Black vs white paper home lab** (◇): dark surfaces absorb more light. The script says "often" for dark solar panels, because not all are dark.
- **Book facts:**
  - Solar panels are tubes of water that heat up. Solar cells turn light into electricity directly; the book photo shows solar cells powering Europa-Park in Rust, Germany. Solar power stations use mirrors to reflect sunlight onto a tower, making steam.
  - Wind turbines would need to cover an area the size of 53 000 football pitches to produce the same energy as one nuclear power station (book Fact).
  - Only hydroelectricity and geothermal are available at any time, but only in places with a reservoir or hot rocks.
  - Nearly all energy came from the Sun. Only nuclear, tidal and geothermal don't depend on it. A plane has flown on solar power (book Fact).
- ◇ **"Panels Pour, Cells Create"**, **"Hydro and Geo go any time"** and **"The Sun's three rebels: Tides, Toasty rocks, Tiny atoms"** are Volty-made tricks.
- **Safety:** never look at the Sun. Never leave a magnifying glass in sunlight.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Id Other energy resources (p144–145). Celly School's fourth lesson of 7I Energy.
- **Size:** 151 spoken lines (Volty 137, Bolt 14), 31 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1540 words. That is roughly 19–20 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice ⚑ to choose).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ga.

**World:** the Sun Map. A giant golden Sun above Joule Park sends light pathways to every corner: to plants, the fuel depot, clouds and rain, wind and waves. Three rebel attractions glow a different colour, because they don't depend on the Sun: the Tidal Flume, the Geothermal Hot Spring and the Nuclear Reactor. A weather dial switches the park from sunny day to calm night.
- **Case:** "The case of the sunshine breakfast" (the 7Ic teaser: "almost all your energy started in one place. Even your breakfast. Where?").
- **Answered at the end:** the Sun. Sunlight gave plants the energy for photosynthesis. The wheat in the cornflakes stored it, and the cow ate grass to make the milk. So breakfast is stored sunshine. Only nuclear, tidal and geothermal energy don't come from the Sun.

**Main tricks:**
- "Panels Pour hot water. Cells Create current." (pour a jug / flick a switch)
- "Hydro and Geo go any time." (wiggle sunshine fingers, then rain fingers)
- "The Sun's three rebels: Tides, Toasty rocks, Tiny atoms." (count three fingers)
