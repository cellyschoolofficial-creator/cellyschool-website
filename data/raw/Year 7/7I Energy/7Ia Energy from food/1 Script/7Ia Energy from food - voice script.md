# 7Ia Energy from food — voice script

**Check before recording (flags)**
- ⚑ **Host for physics:** the brand doc names **Volty the spark** as the physics hero (7I–7L). This script makes Volty the host and keeps Bolt as the sidekick. Volty has no recorded voice yet. Pick a voice before recording; Zephyr is Atomy's voice and Puck is Bolt's. Volty also needs a new intro bumper ("Meet Volty, our physics hero") and a raspberry physics theme.
- ⚑ **Lavoisier and Laplace's guinea pig** (true story, Ia10): this is accurate but beyond the book ◇. In the early 1780s (about 1782–83) they put a guinea pig in a container packed with ice. They measured how much ice its body heat melted and compared that with burning charcoal. The script says "in the seventeen-eighties", gives no amounts, and says only that the guinea pig "sat inside" (the earlier "calm and safe" was cut, because it is not recorded either way).
- ⚑ **Koala and climber** (hook, Ia00, Ia12): the book says koalas sleep for around 20 hours a day because eucalyptus leaves don't give much energy, and that mountain climbers need food giving about 19 000 kJ a day. The script uses exactly these.
- **Food labels (book A):** a bun (65 g) has 544 kJ, or 837 kJ per 100 g. A falafel burger (125 g) has 1990 kJ, or 1592 kJ per 100 g. Two burgers have 3980 kJ (answers).
- **Burning food test (book):** this is a school lab practical. Wear eye protection, don't eat the foods, and don't use nuts. The script never suggests trying it at home.
- **Fair test:** 4 g of cheese raised the water 16 °C, so 4 °C per gram. Bread gave 2 °C per gram, so bread : cheese = 1 : 2. Diet crispbread gave 1.0 °C per gram, so crispbread : bread = 1 : 2. Pears store 175 kJ per 100 g and bananas 350 kJ, so 1 : 2 (book Q).
- ⚑ **Order of energy needs (Ia04_08–09):** the book asks for baby, fire-fighter, secretary and 11-year-old in order, but the answer key wasn't checked. The script gives baby, eleven-year-old, office worker, fire-fighter. A growing 11-year-old and a desk worker are close, so check the Activity Pack answer before recording. "Office worker" replaces the book's "secretary".
- **Energy balance** is kept neutral and body-positive: no weighing scales and no "good" or "bad" bodies.
- ◇ **kcal:** labels also show kcal. The script says once that it's "another unit for energy" and that scientists use joules.
- ◇ **"Grow. Go. Glow."**, **"Kilo keeps a thousand"** and **"Per gram is fair"** are Volty-made tricks.

Pearson Exploring Science International, Year 7, Student Book 7Ia Energy from food (p133–135) and 7Ia Comparing energy in foods (Working Scientifically, p136–137). Celly School's first lesson of 7I Energy, and the first physics lesson.
- **Size:** 153 spoken lines (Volty 139, Bolt 14), 31 long pauses, 13 scenes (hook + 12), 2 parts, about 1590 words. That is roughly 19–20 minutes with pauses.
- **Host:** Volty the spark (voice ⚑ to choose).
- **Sidekick:** Bolt the robot (voice Puck).
- **Sentence length:** 15 words or fewer.

**Lesson grammar:** same as 7Ga.

**World:** Joule Park opens. It's a theme park where every ride, snack stall and visitor has labelled energy jars. Energy is counted in golden Joule tokens. At the gate stands the Counting Clock. In 7Ia, every visitor has a Chemical Jar in their tummy, filled at the food stalls. Each stall has a giant glowing nutrition label.
- **Case:** "The case of the sleepy koala" (the 7He teaser: "Where does the energy in your breakfast go?").
- **Answered at the end:** eucalyptus leaves don't give much energy, so koalas sleep about 20 hours a day to save it. Climbers move all day and keep warm in the cold, so they need about 19 000 kilojoules a day.

**Main tricks:**
- "Grow. Go. Glow." Energy from food lets you grow and repair, move, and keep warm. (stretch tall / run on the spot / hug yourself)
- "Kilo keeps a thousand." 1 kilojoule = 1000 joules. (flick fingers three times: zero, zero, zero)
- "Per gram is fair." Divide by the mass to compare foods fairly.
