Help for grown-ups

How to help a dyslexic child remember long science words

Long science words stick best when a child hears them, says them, taps them and sees them linked to a picture, all at once. Break each word into beats (one tap per syllable), spot any useful word parts, add a memory picture, and practise the same few words again two or three days later.

A five-step routine for each new word

  1. Say it slowly. You say the word, your child says it back. No spelling yet.
  2. Tap the beats. One tap on the table for each syllable: chlo·ro·phyll is three taps.
  3. Find the parts. Many science words are built from pieces: photo (light) + synthesis (making). Knowing the pieces helps with new words too.
  4. Link a picture. Draw it, act it out, or connect it to something familiar (chloroplasts are the green "solar panels" of a leaf cell).
  5. Use it again later. Come back after two or three days and once more the next week.

Memory tricks that work

Silly sentences for lists, such as MRS GREN for the life processes (movement, respiration, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion, nutrition).

One big picture to hang a topic on: a cell as a school, with the nucleus as the head teacher's office.

Colour and gesture: give each word a colour or a hand movement.

What to avoid

Long spelling lists to copy out. Copying is slow and does little for memory.

Too many words at once. Three to five new words in a session is plenty.

Questions parents ask

What is syllable tapping?

Saying a word slowly and tapping once for each syllable (each beat). It helps a child hear the parts of a long word, which makes it easier to say, spell and remember.

How many science words should we practise at once?

Three to five new words in a session, then revisit them a few days later. Small, repeated practice works better than long lists.

Do memory tricks really help dyslexic children?

Many families and teachers find them very useful. Linking a word to a picture, a sound pattern or a funny sentence gives the brain more than one way to find it again.
