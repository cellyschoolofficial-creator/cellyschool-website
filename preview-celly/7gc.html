Key words

Say each word slowly and tap one beat for each part. Tapping the beats helps you spell and remember long science words.

Brownian motion

BrownIAnMoTion

the jerky, random movement of small specks, caused by being hit by moving particles of a liquid or gas

random

RanDom

with no pattern; you can't tell which way it will go next

evidence

EvIDence

observations or data that show whether an idea is correct

nanometre

NanOMeTre

a billionth of a metre: 0.000 000 001 m

Memory tricks

Random hits, Random path.
Brownian means Bumped, not Brave.
Nano knows nine zeros. Flash nine fingers, then tap!

Three big ideas

  1. Brownian motion is the random, jerky movement of tiny specks.
  2. It happens because invisible moving particles hit the speck unevenly.
  3. Brownian motion was strong evidence for the particle theory.

Quick quiz

Say your answer out loud first, then check.

What is Brownian motion?

The jerky, random movement of tiny specks, caused by moving particles hitting them.

Who first saw it, with pollen in water?

Robert Brown, in eighteen twenty-seven.

Why does the speck's direction keep changing?

The particles hit it randomly. Random hits, Random path.

Who explained it, and who tested the prediction?

Einstein explained it. Perrin tested it.

How many nanometres are in a metre?

A billion! Nano knows nine zeros.
