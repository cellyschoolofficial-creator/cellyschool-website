Key words

Say each word slowly and tap one beat for each part. Tapping the beats helps you spell and remember long science words.

indicator

InDiCaTor

a substance that changes colour in acids and alkalis

litmus

LitMus

an indicator made from lichen: red in acid, blue in alkali

alkali

AlKaLi

a substance that feels soapy and turns litmus blue

neutral

NeuTral

neither acidic nor alkaline; does not change indicator colours

phenolphthalein

FeenOlFthayLeen

an indicator: colourless in acid, pink in alkali

Memory tricks

Acid makes it Red. Alkali makes it Blue.
Al-kali means Ashes. Rub your hands, like washing with soap.
Phenolphthalein Pinks in alkali. Plain in acid.

Three big ideas

  1. Indicators change colour in acids and alkalis. Acid makes it Red. Alkali makes it Blue.
  2. Many alkalis are corrosive, especially in your eyes. Neutral is Not always safe.
  3. Indicators tell us acid or alkali. But not how strong.

Quick quiz

Say your answer out loud first, then check.

What is an indicator?

A substance that changes colour in acids and alkalis.

What colour do acids turn litmus?

Red! Acid makes it Red.

Where does the word alkali come from?

From al quali. Arabic for ashes, used to make the first soap.

Pure water is neutral. Does it change litmus?

No! Neutral solutions do not change indicator colours.

Are all neutral substances safe?

No! Some are toxic. Neutral is Not always safe.
