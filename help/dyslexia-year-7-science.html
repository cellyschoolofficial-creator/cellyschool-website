How to help a dyslexic child with Year 7 science at home

Dyslexia makes the reading and spelling side of science hard, not the science itself. Help at home works best in short, calm sessions: watch one short part of a lesson, say and tap the new words, then do one small practice task. Repeat little and often, and keep the focus on understanding rather than writing.

Why Year 7 science feels hard

Year 7 brings a flood of long new words: mitochondria, chromatography, neutralisation. A dyslexic child may understand the idea in class but lose the word by the next day.

Textbook pages are dense, and homework often asks for written answers. The difficulty is usually with reading, spelling and memory for words, not with thinking scientifically.

Seven steps that help at home

  1. Keep sessions short: 15 to 20 minutes, then stop while it is still going well.
  2. Watch first, read second. A short video part gives the big picture before any reading.
  3. Teach the key words out loud. Say each word slowly and tap one beat per syllable (mi·to·chon·dri·a). Then link it to a picture.
  4. Use memory tricks: a silly sentence for a list, or one picture to hang ideas on (for example, a cell as a school).
  5. Do one small practice task: label a picture, sort cards, or answer two questions out loud.
  6. Ask your child to teach it back to you in their own words. Explaining is strong practice.
  7. Come back to the same words in two or three days. Short repeats beat one long session.

What to say to school

Ask whether key-word lists can be sent home before a new topic, and whether answers can sometimes be given as labelled diagrams, bullet points or out loud.

Ask about coloured paper, larger print and extra time in tests, in line with the school's SEN support.

Questions parents ask

Is dyslexia a sign that my child is not good at science?

No. Dyslexia mainly affects reading, spelling and working with the sounds of words. Many dyslexic children are strong at seeing patterns, building things and asking big questions, which is what science is about.

How long should science homework take for a dyslexic child?

Keep it short and focused: about 15 to 20 minutes for practice at home is plenty. If written homework takes much longer than the teacher expects, it is worth telling the school.

Should my child read the textbook first or watch a video?

For most dyslexic learners, watching a short explanation first works better. It gives the big picture and the sound of the new words before they meet them in print.
